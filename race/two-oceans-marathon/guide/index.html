Two Oceans Marathon

Cape Town, Western Cape · South Africa
UltraCoastalUltra Marathon · Half MarathonEntries open

Fifty-six kilometres around the Cape Peninsula, with both of its famous climbs saved for after the marathon mark.

Quick verdict

The prettiest fifty-six kilometres in running, structured to punish anyone who enjoys the first twenty-eight. Chapman's Peak at 28 km, Constantia Nek at 42, and a seven-hour limit with three gates inside it. You need a sub-five marathon just to stand on the line.

Course at a glance

56 kmDistance
7 hrsCutoff
3Cut-off gates

An anticlockwise loop of the peninsula from Newlands to UCT. Down the False Bay coast through Muizenberg, Kalk Bay and Fish Hoek, across to Noordhoek, up and over Chapman's Peak, into Hout Bay, then the long climb of Constantia Nek before nine downhill kilometres past Kirstenbosch to the finish.

Where you'll feel it

📍Constantia Nek, from 42 kilometres.

Chapman's Peak is the photograph; Constantia Nek is the race. It starts at almost exactly marathon distance, after the Peak has already emptied your legs, and the 42.2 km gate sits at the bottom of it — so you arrive under time pressure and then climb.

Best chance to push

From the top of the Nek at around 46 kilometres. Nine rolling downhill kilometres through Constantia and past Kirstenbosch into UCT — fast if anything is left, and brutal on quads if it is not.

Three things to know

  1. Log your qualifier, don't just run it. The 56 km needs a sub-5:00 marathon or equivalent, submitted to your profile by a hard deadline months out. People lose entries to the paperwork every year, not to the running.
  2. If you are not South African you cannot enter the ballot and do not need to. International places go through Marathon Tours on a first-come basis — no lottery, but you must pay before capacity fills.
  3. Pace the flat first twenty-eight kilometres to bank the 42.2 km gate. Expect to lose forty to fifty seconds a kilometre on the Nek, and note the two published cutoff definitions disagree — if you are in an early batch, run to the stricter one.

Race-day weather

5:15amStart
21,000Runners

Early April in the Cape: around 14°C at a quarter past five, rising into the low twenties by late morning. The south-easter is at the tail of its season, but a southerly on the day means a headwind across Chapman's Peak, which is exactly where you least want one.

What runners wear

Standard road kit; this is a mild autumn race, not a cold one. A throwaway top for the 05:15 start. Nothing underfoot needs to be special — it is tar the whole way.

Typical finish times

Thompson Magawana's 3:03:44 has stood since 1988. Gerda Steyn holds the women's record at 3:26:54 and has won seven in a row. About ten and a half thousand finish the 56 km, and with a sub-five marathon as the entry floor most are compressed between five and seven hours.

Worth the trip?

It has called itself the world's most beautiful marathon since long before that was a marketing line, and Chapman's Peak closed to traffic justifies it. But this is South Africa's ultra culture, not a tourist event: you qualify, you climb late, and you start at quarter past five.
