Copenhagen Half Marathon

København, Capital Region of Denmark · Denmark
RoadCityHalf Marathon

A flat, fast loop of Copenhagen: the Marble Church, the harbour front and Dronning Louises Bro, a finish in Fælledparken, and a lottery 188,000 strong.

Quick verdict

A flat, fast loop of Copenhagen, and now one of the hardest halves to get into: 188,000 entered the lottery for 45,000 places, with no waiting list. The men's winner runs about 58 minutes, and the 2019 race set a world record. Pick it if you were drawn, or if a tour package suits you.

Course at a glance

9:30Start
3 hCut-off
58:01Course record

One loop on closed roads, starting on Jagtvej by Fælledparken and finishing inside it. The first half is the old city: down Østerbrogade, past the Botanical Garden and Rosenborg to Kongens Nytorv, a loop up towards Kastellet and back by the Marble Church, then the harbour front past Christiansborg and the Black Diamond to City Hall Square at 10 km. The second half is the neighbourhoods: Frederiksberg Allé, north to Nørrebro, and down Nørrebrogade over Dronning Louises Bro and along the Lakes to the finish.

Where you'll feel it

📍The corners between 4 and 11 km

There is no hill. The old city packs the turns into seven kilometres: a hairpin at Kongens Nytorv, a U-turn near Kastellet and a hook by Tivoli, each one a surge in a crowd of thousands. On the harbour front around 9 km the wind off the water finds you.

Best chance to push

From Nørrebro Station at 16.5 km it is straight road home: down Nørrebrogade, over Dronning Louises Bro, where the crowd is loudest, and along the Lakes to Øster Allé. The 2026 world title was decided in the last 600 metres past Parken.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is by free lottery, and it is huge: 188,000 entered for 45,000 places, with no waiting list. Places drawn but not paid for are drawn again. Otherwise the way in is an official tour-operator package. Entries cannot be transferred, sold or deferred, except for pregnancy.
  2. Collect your bib at the expo in the days before, with your race card and photo ID. Bag drop is in Fælledparken, beside the finish. The metro and S-trains run as normal on race day, so take them: roads across the city are closed.
  3. Five refreshment zones, each with water, an energy drink, showers, toilets and Vaseline, and pacers with coloured balloons from 1:25 to 2:30 every five minutes. Start groups go by expected time from 9:30. The limit is three hours, net.

Race-day weather

Mild, often bright, sometimes wet: mid-September runs about 11 to 17°C. The 2025 race started in heavy rain, and in 2017 a thunderstorm stopped it. Expect a breeze on the harbour front.

What runners wear

Singlet and shorts, a throwaway layer for the start group, and shoes with grip: painted road lines turn slick in the rain, as the 2025 leader found at 7 km.

Typical finish times

Course records 58:01 by Geoffrey Kamworor in 2019, the world record then, and 1:04:44 by Likina Amebaw in 2025. On today's course the men have won in 58:05 and 58:23. Behind them 31,743 started in 2025, 42 per cent women, average age 37.

Worth the trip?

Yes, if the lottery picks you: a flat, fast half through a city that turns out for it, from the Marble Church to Dronning Louises Bro, finishing in a park. The odds are the catch, fewer than one in four, so have a plan B for September.
