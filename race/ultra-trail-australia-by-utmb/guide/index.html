Ultra-Trail Australia

Blue Mountains, New South Wales · Australia
TrailMountainSold out

Stairs, sandstone and the Jamison Valley — Australia's biggest trail weekend ends with 951 steps back to the top.

Quick verdict

Australia's flagship trail festival and the World Series' Oceania Major: nearly 8,000 runners across six races beneath the Blue Mountains escarpment. The UTA100 is the classic — 101 km of ladders, valleys and staircases that finishes UP the 951 Furber Steps.

Course at a glance

4,400mClimbing
28 hrsCutoff
951 stepsFinish

From Scenic World the 100 runs out along Narrow Neck and down the Tarros Ladders — metal rungs bolted to the cliff — through the Megalong Valley to the Six Foot Track, then up Nellies Glen's stairs back to Katoomba. Cliff walks past Echo Point and Leura Forest lead to Kings Tableland, the Kedumba Valley, and the Furber Steps.

Where you'll feel it

📍Out of the Kedumba Valley, with the Furber Steps still waiting.

The last big climb starts on the valley floor with around 80 km behind you — and the course's final word is 951 stairs from the Jamison Valley up to the finish line at Scenic World. Trained stair legs are the difference between a finish photo and a crawl.

Best chance to push

Off Kings Tableland, roughly 78 to 88 km, the track turns smooth, wide and gently down — the one stretch built for opening up before Kedumba closes everything down again.

Three things to know

  1. No ballot and no qualifier — but entries open around mid-September and the 100 has sold out within a day before now. A UTMB Index buys a 48-hour head start, and the Miler banks eight Running Stones plus a Western States lottery ticket for a sub-42-hour finish.
  2. The gear culture is famously strict: taped-seam jacket, thermals, gloves, hi-vis after dark — checked before you collect your bib and randomly on course, with time penalties. Treat the list as training, not admin.
  3. Katoomba is 2½ hours by train from Sydney Central with a free shuttle to Scenic World — but beds book out festival-wide, so sort accommodation with your entry. Crews may help at checkpoints only on the 100 and Miler; the shorter races allow none.

Autumn in the mountains: roughly 5–16°C, frosty pre-dawn starts and valley fog that burns off to clear escarpment days. The cold is front-loaded — you shiver in the start pen and shed layers by Narrow Neck.

What runners wear

Thermals and gloves for the 5 a.m. waves, singlet weather by mid-morning, and the exact mandatory jacket — fully taped seams, hooded — in the pack all day. Shoes with grip for stone steps that spend half the year damp.

Typical finish times

Michael Dunstan won the 2026 UTA100 in 9:04, and the 28-hour limit brought 84% of starters home — 1,387 of 1,652. The Miler is another animal entirely: 7,200 m of climbing against a 44½-hour clock.

Worth the trip?

For southern-hemisphere runners this is the pilgrimage: the scale of a Major with the Three Sisters as backdrop, kids' races to milers on one weekend, and a finish line you climb to. Book the bed the day you enter.
