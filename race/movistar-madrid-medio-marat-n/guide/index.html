Madrid Half Marathon

Madrid · Spain
RoadUrbanMedio Maratón · 5KEntries open

Madrid in one loop: up the Castellana to the leaning KIO towers, round the Puerta de Alcalá, down the Gran Vía and through Sol to a finish by Cibeles.

Quick verdict

A half that shows you Madrid: one loop from Recoletos up the Castellana to the leaning KIO towers, then back through the Puerta de Alcalá, the Gran Vía and Sol to a finish by Cibeles. 28,000 ran the last edition, and it sold out four weeks early. Pick it for the city; Barcelona and Valencia are the PB courses.

Course at a glance

+80 mFirst 5 km
9:15Start
3 hCut-off

One loop on closed roads, starting and finishing on Paseo de Recoletos by the National Library. The first five kilometres climb the Castellana past the Bernabéu to turn under the KIO towers at Plaza de Castilla. Bravo Murillo runs back down to Cuatro Caminos, then the course crosses east to skirt the Retiro on O'Donnell, circles the Puerta de Alcalá at 14 km and climbs Serrano. From 18 km it runs the Gran Vía, Sol and the Congreso to Neptuno, and up past Cibeles to the line. The 5K starts half an hour earlier by Colón.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Castellana to Plaza de Castilla, kilometres 2.5 to 5

The race front-loads its climb: about 80 metres in the first five kilometres, the steepest pitches past Nuevos Ministerios and just before the KIO towers. It hurts because you are fresh and want to go. After that the stings are short: O'Donnell, Serrano, and the Gran Vía up to Callao.

Best chance to push

Bravo Murillo, kilometres 5 to 8, gives back much of the early climb in one long descent to Cuatro Caminos. The second gift is 18 to 20 km: down from the Gran Vía through Sol and past the Congreso to Neptuno, before a gentle rise to the line.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is first come, first served in price tiers, with no ballot, and it has sold out every year lately, last time four weeks before race day. Official name changes by email until 28 February, then at the expo, for a fee. No deferrals; refunds only with the optional guarantee.
  2. Bibs are handed out only at the expo, Thursday to Saturday; nothing on race morning. Bag drop is free in Plaza de Colón from eight. Pens go by your time, and anything faster than 1:30 needs an official half from the last two years. Take the Metro to Colón, Serrano or Retiro.
  3. Water at about 5, 10 and 15 km in small bottles, and pace groups every five minutes from 1:20 to 2:10. The cut-off is three hours. There are no showers at the finish; your bag is a short walk away in Colón.

Race-day weather

28,000Field

Cool and usually bright: early April averages 8°C at dawn and 18 in the afternoon, so expect about 10 at the 9:15 start and the mid-teens by the finish, 650 metres up. Rain falls on about one day in four.

What runners wear

Short sleeves and shorts, a throwaway layer for the pen in the cool of the morning, and road shoes you trust on the early climb and the long descents.

Typical finish times

Course records 59:38 by Ronald Kirui in 2021 and 1:07:22 by Winfridah Moseti in 2022, both on an earlier course. The 2026 winners ran 1:00:50 and 1:08:31. Behind them 28,000 ran the half, a third of them women and more than a third from abroad.

Worth the trip?

Yes, for Madrid's monuments in one morning on closed roads: the Castellana, the Puerta de Alcalá, the Gran Vía, Sol and Cibeles, with runners from nearly 100 countries. Come for the city rather than a best time: the climb comes early and the corners come late.
