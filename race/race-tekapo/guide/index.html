Race Tekapo

Lake Tekapo, Canterbury · New Zealand
TrailLake

Turquoise water, a dark-sky dawn and the climb to the observatory — the Mackenzie's trail day out, 2 km to 50.

Quick verdict

Canterbury's biggest trail day and the friendliest way into the Mackenzie: seven distances from a kids' dash to a 50 km ultra that starts under the stars of a dark-sky reserve and summits Mt John twice. Small town, big sky.

Course at a glance

~1,000mClimbing
6:00amStart
7Races

The 50 km is a figure-of-eight from the lakefront: over the footbridge past the Church of the Good Shepherd to Sawdon Station and the Lagoon Loop, then up Mt John past the observatory, down to the Peninsula Walkway and out by Lakes McGregor and Alexandrina — before climbing Mt John a second time and dropping home along the water.

Where you'll feel it

📍Mt John — twice, on the 50 km.

The climb behind Tekapo Springs is sharp enough the first time; the second ascent comes past 40 km with the 2 p.m. checkpoint at its back. The summit pays in full — 360 degrees of southern alps, Tekapo and Alexandrina — then hands you the descent home.

Best chance to push

The Regional Park: soft forest trails and flat lakeside kilometres open and close the day. On the 16 km they are the whole race — it is the one course that skips Mt John entirely.

Three things to know

  1. Every distance runs the same Saturday, spread from the ultra's 6 a.m. dark start to a 1 p.m. 5 km — a family can fill the whole day. Entries stay open to the Wednesday before at 8 p.m., with day-of entry if space remains.
  2. From 12 km up the gear list is alpine and checked: thermal top, seam-sealed jacket, hat, gloves, survival bag. The village sits at 700 m and September nights freeze; the 50 km adds a headlamp for the starlit first hour and a spare dry thermal.
  3. The 50 km is an official UTMB Index race — a finish here banks the Index that opens UTMB World Series entries. Cutoffs are checkpoint sweeps: 41 km by 2 p.m. on the ultra, 26 km by 2 p.m. on the 32.

Early spring in the Mackenzie is honest alpine weather: daytime highs around 9°C, most September nights below freezing, snow never quite off the table. The dark-sky dawn is magic precisely because the air is that clear — and that cold.

What runners wear

Start in the full thermal kit and shed as the sun gets up — the 6 a.m. start can be well below zero. Trail shoes with grip for tussock and station tracks, and pack exactly the list: checks are random and the rule is no gear, no race.

Worth the trip?

As a destination race day, absolutely: the turquoise is real, the observatory summit is a genuine reward, and seven distances mean nobody in the car is a spectator. A young event wearing its ambition well — book a bed early, the township is tiny.
