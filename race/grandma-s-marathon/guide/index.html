Grandma’s Marathon

Duluth, Minnesota · United States
RoadLakeMarathon · Half Marathon · 5KSold out

America’s best-loved point-to-point — down the North Shore of Lake Superior into a city that turns race day into a holiday.

Quick verdict

A genuine PB course wrapped in a small-town festival. Net downhill 40 m, cool lake air at your shoulder most of the way, and a finish Duluth turns out for like a state fair. Be ready the evening registration opens — the 50th running sold out in about 12 hours.

Course at a glance

↓40mNet
169mClimbing
7 hrsCutoff

From Two Harbors the course runs one road — Scenic Highway 61 — southwest along Lake Superior into Duluth. The first 8 miles drift gently downhill, miles 9–16 roll, and from Lester River the crowds build through London Road to the Canal Park finish. One late climb at mile 22 is all the course asks.

Where you'll feel it

📍Lemon Drop Hill, mile 22.

Ten vertical metres — a nothing number after 21 miles of gentle rhythm, which is exactly why it bites. The climb off London Road is short, sharp and arrives precisely when your quads are done being fooled by the early descent. Crest it and the race tips downhill to the finish.

Best chance to push

Not the opening descent — that pace feels free and is quietly expensive. The course gives back over the last four miles: after Lemon Drop it runs flat-to-down through downtown crowds loud enough to carry you home.

Three things to know

  1. Registration opens at 7:00 p.m. Central on 1 October, first-come — and the fee is lowest ($155) for only the first five days. Treat the opening like a race start: account made, card ready.
  2. Point-to-point means a bus: 5:00–5:45 a.m. from six Duluth staging points, limited seats, first-come. Set the alarm for 4-something and bring a warm layer you can abandon.
  3. Duluth’s beds sell out months ahead of its 87,000 population’s biggest weekend — book accommodation the night you enter, or look across the bridge in Superior, Wisconsin.

Lake Superior is the course’s air-conditioning — 14°C on an average race morning, humid, sometimes foggy. The historic range runs 11–19°C, and the warm years punish anyone who spent the cool early miles overdrawing.

What runners wear

A throwaway layer for the bus and the Two Harbors wait, then singlet and shorts. A cap earns its place — Scenic 61 is open road with little shade once the sun clears the lake. Use the drop-bag; the finish by the water can be cool.

Typical finish times

Winners run 2:09–2:11 — the course record has stood at 2:09:06 since 2014. The road stays open a full 7 hours and the field uses all of it; this is a race that celebrates its last finisher as loudly as its first.

Worth the trip?

For Americans chasing a fast course with soul, unreservedly. For international runners, Duluth is a long way to come — and that remoteness is the charm: a whole small city given over to one race, on a shoreline that looks like the sea. Yes, with a 12-hour entry window’s worth of planning.
