Cape Town Marathon

Cape Town, Western Cape · South Africa
RoadCityMarathon · 10K Peace RunEntries open

Africa's first Abbott World Marathon Major — and the fastest marathon ever run on the continent.

Quick verdict

The eighth Major, and it takes the title at this very edition. Flat, quick, sea-level at both ends, and the only Major on earth with no qualifying time at all. The catch is the wind, and it waits until the last eight kilometres.

Course at a glance

53mHigh point
km 24–27Crux climb
6 h 30Cutoff

A city loop from Green Point past the V&A Waterfront and the Foreshore, into the city bowl by the Castle and City Hall, south through Woodstock and Observatory to the UCT corridor beneath Table Mountain, then back to the Atlantic for a flat finish along the Sea Point Promenade. The high point is just 53 metres.

Where you'll feel it

📍The UCT corridor, kilometres 24 to 27.

It is not steep — the whole course tops out at 53 metres — but it is long, it grinds up against Table Mountain, and everything before it is flat. Runners who race the opening twenty-four kilometres get the bill here.

Best chance to push

The last ten kilometres along the Sea Point Promenade: flat, sea level and smooth, with the city bowl behind you. On a still day it is the best closing stretch in the series.

Three things to know

  1. The 2027 ballot closed in June 2026. If you do not already have a place, the routes in are a charity partner or one of eighty-plus official tour operators, both first come until sold out. The race has sold out three years running.
  2. It is the only Major with no qualifying time whatsoever, and the ballot costs five rand. That makes it the most accessible Major in the world on paper — the barrier is the flight, not the finish time.
  3. Do not bank on a free last ten kilometres. That promenade is unsheltered Atlantic frontage, Cape Town averages about seventeen kilometres an hour of wind in May, and the 2025 race was cancelled outright after overnight winds wrecked the course.

Race-day weather

12–20°CCape autumn
8:10amStart
27,000Runners

May is why the race moved here from October: cooler, and past the worst of the south-easter. Expect 12 to 14°C at the start and high teens by the finish, with wind the only real variable.

What runners wear

Ordinary road racing kit and a throwaway layer for the waves — the first mass wave goes at 08:10 and the last at 08:27. Nothing special underfoot; it is tar and paved promenade throughout.

Typical finish times

Mohamed Esa ran 2:04:55 here in 2026, the fastest marathon ever run on African soil. Glenrose Xaba's 2:22:22 from 2024 still stands. The cutoff is six and a half hours, and your clock depends which of the five waves you start in.

Worth the trip?

The only Major in Africa, on a course quick enough for a personal best, with Table Mountain over your shoulder for the middle third. The flight is long and the wind is a genuine risk — but no other Major lets you in without a qualifying time.
