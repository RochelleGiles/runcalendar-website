JFK 50 Mile

Quick verdict

The oldest ultramarathon in America, born from a presidential dare in 1963 and never missed since. Three races in one: a rocky Appalachian ridge, 26 flat towpath miles that decide everything, and rolling farm roads home. Racing the first third ruins the second.

Course at a glance

13 miAT ridge
26.3 miTowpath
13 hrsCutoff

Point-to-point from Boonsboro to Williamsport in three acts. Road climbs 1,172 ft onto South Mountain, then the Appalachian Trail rolls rocky along the ridge past Gathland Gap before plunging 1,000 ft down the Weverton Cliffs switchbacks. The C&O Canal towpath follows — 26.3 dead-flat gravel miles beside the Potomac — until Dam 4 releases you onto 8.4 miles of rolling road.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Appalachian Trail ridge, miles 2.5 to 15.5.

Not the early 1,172 ft of climb but the footing: rock gardens under November leaves, sometimes iced at dawn. Race it and the 1,000 ft Weverton switchback descent finishes what the rocks started — with 35 flat miles waiting to collect the debt.

Best chance to push

The towpath. From Weverton to Dam 4 there is not a hill worth the name — pick a pace at Antietam Aqueduct and hold it. The front-runners click off metronome miles here; so can you.

Three things to know

  1. No qualifying standard and no lottery — but 2026 sold out in record time after opening on 1 March, with fees stepping from $200 to $300 as places filled. The date is traditionally the Saturday before Thanksgiving; set the March alarm.
  2. One mass start at 6:30 a.m., closed at 6:35, and a 13-hour limit with checkpoints at 5, 7½ and 11 hours. Sunset is before 5 p.m., so slower than roughly 10½-hour pace means finishing in the dark. Headphones are banned.
  3. Crew can meet you at exactly three places — Weverton Cliffs (mile 15), Antietam Aqueduct (27.3) and Taylors Landing (38.7) — with fourteen aid stations between. Buses run finishers back from 3 p.m., and military teams have raced the Kennedy Cup here since the sixties.

Race-day weather

Late November in western Maryland promises nothing: starts here have come at 28°F and in the fifties, and the race has been run through sleet, snow and flood. Bank on a freezing dawn in Boonsboro and treat anything milder as a gift.

What runners wear

Dress for the freezing start in layers you can shed on the towpath: gloves, a hat, a base you would happily run 50°F in. Grippy shoes earn their keep on the ridge — the leaves hide most of the rocks.

Typical finish times

David Sinclair's 5:08:27 record has stood since 2024; recent winners run 5:10–5:30 and the first woman close to six hours. Last year 1,111 of 1,208 starters beat the 13-hour limit — this race gets finished, but the back half finishes in the dark.

Worth the trip?

Not for scenery — for standing. This is the founding start line of American ultrarunning, run every year since 1963, and finishing puts your name in the sport's longest ledger. If you want mountain drama, look at the UTMB races; if you want to run the oldest ultra in America, there is exactly one.
