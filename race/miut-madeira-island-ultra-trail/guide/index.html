Madeira Island Ultra-Trail

Machico, Madeira · Portugal
UltraMountain

Coast to coast across Madeira in one night and a day — laurel forest, hand-cut cliff paths, and the stair-carved traverse between the island's two highest peaks.

Quick verdict

One of Europe's hardest island ultras and the best thing the World Trail Majors calendar does in April: 110 km from Porto Moniz to Machico over 7,200 m of climbing, started at one minute to midnight. The island is small; the race is not.

Course at a glance

7,200mClimbing
32 hrsCutoff
23:59Start

The Legend crosses the island north-west to south-east: a 23:59 start in Porto Moniz, a midnight climb to Fanal's ancient laurel forest, the Paul da Serra plateau at Estanquinhos, Encumeada, the amphitheatre of Curral das Freiras — then the summit chain over 1,862 m Pico Ruivo and Pico do Areeiro before the long cliff-path run down Boca do Risco to Machico.

Where you'll feel it

📍Pico Ruivo to Pico do Areeiro, around 85 km in.

The stair-carved traverse between the island's two highest points arrives with 85 km in your legs — exposed, relentless, rebuilt after the 2024 fires and no gentler for it. The stairs off Areeiro ask for an honest jump down. Budget hours here, not minutes.

Best chance to push

The last 15 km. Off Portela the course drops to the Boca do Risco cliff path and the Levada do Caniçal — ocean on your left, gravity on your side — and finally lets you run, all the way into Machico.

Three things to know

  1. Places go 90% first-come and 10% by lottery, opening race by race in late October — and every 2026 distance sold out. The back doors are a €300 Solidarity Bib or one of twenty Travel Bibs. The Legend asks for 4 ITRA points, the Marathon and Starter for none — the honest first taste of Madeira's staircases.
  2. The start is a shuttle ride: organiser buses leave for Porto Moniz late on race eve, arriving about an hour before the midnight gun. Everything finishes at Fórum Machico on the opposite coast — ten minutes from the airport.
  3. The Legend and Advanced demand TWO headlamps with spares plus a red rear light, and kit is checked: waterproof, survival blanket, bandage, whistle, cup. The summits make their own weather and the rules are written for it.

Race-day weather

~19°CCoast
~6°CSummits
2Headlamps

Late April is mild at sea level — around 19°C — and a different planet up high: Pico do Areeiro averages 6°C in April, and fog, wind and rain build above 1,000 m in minutes. Recent editions met 5°C summits with visibility down to metres.

What runners wear

Night layers you can shed on the plateau, with a warm layer kept reachable for the summit chain — the traverse is slow, and slow means cold. Grippy shoes with real toe protection: the stairs are stone, wet more often than not.

Typical finish times

Vincent Esmiol won the 2026 Legend in 12:48, Katharina Hartmuth in 15:50; a solid mid-packer is out beyond 20 hours, and the 32-hour cutoff means the tail runs into a second sunrise. Around 580 finished the Legend last year.

Worth the trip?

For mountain runners, absolutely — this is the island ultra the others get measured against, through a UNESCO laurel forest older than the towns below it. Fly into Funchal and finish ten minutes from the airport. Just respect the calendar: October sells the places, April runs them.
