Daytona Beach Half Marathon

Daytona Beach, Florida · United States
RoadRiverHalf Marathon · 5KEntries open

A half that starts with a lap of Daytona International Speedway, runs out to the Halifax River and finishes in Victory Circle.

Quick verdict

A half marathon that spends its first three miles inside Daytona International Speedway, then runs out to the Halifax River and home to Victory Circle. About 1,500 run the morning, and the 5K, one lap of the track, has sold out before. Flat, cool at dawn and generous with its cut-off.

Course at a glance

First 3 miSpeedway lap
3 h 45Cut-off
Victory CircleFinish

You start at Gate 7 and spend just over three miles on a lap of the Speedway, to the relay's first hand-off outside the turn 4 tunnel. Then east to Beach Street, through Bethune-Cookman University, along the Riverfront Esplanade by the Halifax River, under the Speedway bridge and behind Jackie Robinson Ballpark, and home through the old neighbourhoods to Victory Circle at One Daytona. The 5K is the lap, then the footbridge to the same finish.

Where you'll feel it

📍The run home from the river, miles 8 to 13.

There is no hill to speak of. The test is the last five miles, from the Esplanade back to One Daytona, with the sun up and the roads only partly closed. The cut-off is about 3 hours 45, roughly 17 minutes a mile, and traffic control can enforce it.

Best chance to push

The Speedway lap: the widest, smoothest surface you will ever race on, in the coolest part of the morning. Settle into pace there, then use the flat Riverfront Esplanade to start passing; Victory Circle is worth saving something for.

Three things to know

  1. The entry price steps up on set dates through the winter, with a last step in early January, and there are no refunds: the entry page offers race insurance instead. No transfer or deferral policy is published, so ask the organiser before you count on one.
  2. Packet pickup, parking and the finish all sit at One Daytona, across the boulevard from the track, and you walk to the start at Gate 7 along Daytona Boulevard. Leave time: the half goes off around sunrise, and the 5K does not allow late starts.
  3. The roads are traffic-controlled rather than fully closed, so stay inside the cones. Aid stations have carried water, sports drink, gels and bananas; drop litter only within about 100 metres of each. Every finisher gets a medal and a cold drink at Victory Circle.

Cool at the start and mild by the finish: about 11°C at seven in the morning and around 17°C by ten, with a light north-westerly at dawn. February highs reach 22°C and lows 11°C, and rain falls on about one day in four.

What runners wear

Shorts and a singlet or tee, with a throwaway layer for the walk to Gate 7 in the half-light. Add a cap and sunscreen for the second half, when the sun is well up.

Typical finish times

The front is small and relaxed: half winners have run 1:14 to 1:20 for men and about 1:28 to 1:30 for women, and 5K winners 17 to 19 minutes. The half's cut-off is about 3 hours 45; the 5K has none. Relay teams race for the fun of it, not for awards.

Worth the trip?

Yes, if the track is the reason: a lap of Daytona at dawn is the whole point, and February here feels like a holiday. Despite the name, the course never reaches the beach. Book a room early: the race falls in the run-up to the Daytona 500.
