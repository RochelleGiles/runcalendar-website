Loch Ness Marathon

Inverness, Scotland · United Kingdom
RoadLochMarathon · 10K · 5K · Kids 800mEntries open

Scotland's great point-to-point — a runner's honest guide to a course that gives away 600 metres of descent and still finds a hill at mile 17.

Quick verdict

A net 300-metre drop from moorland to sea level, and still not an easy day — the hill at mile 17 makes sure of that. Come for the Highlands, not for the PB. Respect the first six miles or the last six will punish you.

Course at a glance

310mClimbing
Net ↓304mDescent
7 hrsCutoff

Point-to-point down the south-eastern shore: an exposed moorland start between Whitebridge and Fort Augustus, along General Wade's military road through Foyers and Inverfarigaig, past the beach at Dores, then away from the water and over the hill to Scaniport before crossing the River Ness to finish at Bught Park. You start at 310 metres and finish at five.

Where you'll feel it

📍Aldourie to Scaniport, miles 17–19.

The loch disappears, the shelter goes with it, and the road climbs for the best part of two miles — arriving exactly when a marathon starts to hurt. Everyone who runs Loch Ness talks about this hill. Bank nothing on the long early descent; you'll want those legs here.

Best chance to push

Past 19 miles it levels and tips down towards Scaniport, and the run along the river into Inverness is flat. If you climbed sensibly, the last 10k are the fastest of your day.

Three things to know

  1. The first six miles fall away downhill and it feels free — it isn't. Quad damage banked there gets collected at mile 17, and the organisers' own advice is to make your last six miles the best, not your first.
  2. You can't drive to the start. Every runner is bussed from Inverness Ice Centre — arrive 07:00–07:30 for a 10:00 gun, an hour on a coach, then a long wait on open moorland with no shelter.
  3. 2026 sold out three months earlier than the year before, but 30-plus official charity partners still hold places. Save it as Interested and we'll tell you the moment entry opens for next year.

Race-day weather

7–13°CHighland autumn
10:00amStart
1 hourCoach to start

Highland late September: typically 7–13°C, often grey, occasionally all four seasons in one morning. The start field is open moor with no shelter at all, and you'll be standing in it the best part of an hour before the gun.

What runners wear

Shorts and a long-sleeve once you're moving — but the wait on the moor is the problem, not the race. A throwaway layer you can bin at the start is essential kit. Note the rule: bone-conduction headphones only, ordinary earbuds are banned.

Typical finish times

The course record has stood since 2010 at 2:20:50, with the women's at 2:42:04. Around 3,500 finish. The 7-hour cutoff is firm — the course starts reopening at 16:30, and anyone past six and a half hours finishes on the pavements through Inverness.

Worth the trip?

If you want a marathon that looks like nowhere else, yes. It isn't fast despite the drop, the logistics are a real commitment, and you'll spend a cold hour on a moor before you run a step. You'll also spend three hours running beside Loch Ness. Nobody comes back complaining.
