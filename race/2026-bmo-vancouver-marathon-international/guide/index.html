Vancouver Marathon

Vancouver, British Columbia · Canada
RoadCoastalEntries open

Canada's most beautiful big-city race — out through the forest at UBC, along the beaches, over the Burrard Bridge and round the whole Stanley Park Seawall, with the mountains across the water the entire way.

Quick verdict

Canada's biggest race weekend and its prettiest: 25,000 across the day, sixty countries on the start line, and a point-to-point course that spends most of its length beside the water. Most of the field runs the half — and the half gets the Seawall too, after a fast run through downtown. The marathon is rolling and net downhill with one real hill at 9 km, then a bridge at 29, then a flat lap of Stanley Park to finish. Entry is first-come with no waitlist, and every distance has sold out for three years running; the marathon goes first.

Course at a glance

The marathon leaves Queen Elizabeth Park, the highest point of the day, and drops down Cambie and 49th to SW Marine Drive. Camosun Hill at 9 km lifts you into the forest of Pacific Spirit Park, then the road rolls round the UBC campus before the long descent to Spanish Banks — halfway on the beach road, the North Shore mountains straight ahead across the bay. Point Grey Road and Kitsilano bring the Burrard Bridge at 29 km, the last climb of the morning, and from English Bay it is the Inukshuk and the whole Stanley Park Seawall, round Brockton Point and back along Coal Harbour to Denman, Georgia and the finish on West Pender. The half starts in the same park, crosses the Cambie Bridge past BC Place and Science World, loops through Chinatown and Yaletown to Beach Avenue, then takes Lost Lagoon and the Seawall to the same finish. The 8K starts inside Stanley Park and runs the Seawall home.

Where you'll feel it

📍Camosun Hill, kilometres 9–10

Camosun Hill is the hill everyone talks about: 1.2 km from SW Marine Drive up to the forest, about 4% on average and nearer 7% for the steepest 600 metres past the Southlands school, topping out at the 10 km mark. It comes early enough to be run honestly, and the ones who do are the ones still smiling on the Seawall. The second test is quieter: the short climb out of Jericho at 28 km and the Burrard Bridge straight after, long and gradual, at exactly the point the beach kilometres stop feeling free. After the bridge the course is flat, but the Seawall is open to the wind off English Bay and there is nowhere to hide from it.

Best chance to push

The descent from UBC to Spanish Banks, 19 to 24 km, where the road falls away and the beach opens up ahead of you — bank the rhythm there, not the time. Then the Seawall itself from 32 km: flat, fast, lined the whole way, with the skyline across Coal Harbour pulling you round to Denman Street. Climb the bridge on effort rather than pace and the last ten kilometres are the easiest of the day.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is first-come — no ballot, no waitlist, and a full distance stays full. The marathon has filled before Christmas in recent years and the half and 8K by late winter, so the order is enter first, train second. Save it as Interested and the dates stay on your calendar.
  2. Three starts, one finish. The half goes first from Queen Elizabeth Park at 7:00, the marathon at 8:30, the 8K at 9:30 from inside Stanley Park; everyone finishes on West Pender. The marathon has six hours, with 30 km to be reached in four; the half has three. Bibs come from the Expo at the Convention Centre on the Friday or Saturday — there is no race-day pickup.
  3. It is point-to-point, so the Canada Line is your start line: Oakridge–41st for the marathon, King Edward for the half, with a transit ticket in your race pack. Gear check for the two long races happens at the Expo, not the start, so plan what you carry.

Race-day weather

Early May in Vancouver is mild and changeable: around 9°C at the 7:00 half start, climbing through the mid-teens by the time the marathon reaches the Seawall, with the forest shaded and cool and the beaches open to whatever the bay is doing. Roughly two days in five are wet, and the recent race days have had all of it — light rain one year, clear and cool the next, then a warm one that pushed the downtown core past 20°C by early afternoon and slowed the back of the marathon.

What runners wear

A singlet or short sleeves and shorts, with a throwaway layer for the corral in Queen Elizabeth Park, which sits in shade before the sun is over the trees. Arm warmers earn their place on the Seawall, where the wind off English Bay is the one thing the forest had been hiding from you, and a light shell lives in the gear bag if the forecast turns. Road shoes; there is nothing technical about the course.

Typical finish times

The course records stand at 2:14:33 and 2:33:02 for the marathon and 1:02:36 and 1:12:01 for the half, and the winners usually come home around 2:15 and 2:35. Behind them this is a mid-pack race run for the view: the marathon's median sits a little over four hours, with half the field between about 3:50 and 4:55, and the half's median around 2:10, most runners between 1:55 and 2:30. About 7,000 finish the marathon, 11,000 the half and 2,000 the 8K.

Worth the trip?

Yes — for the course, not the clock. The marathon is rolling and net downhill rather than record-fast, but no other city race on the continent gives you forest, beach, a bridge and a full lap of a park on the water in one morning, with snow still on the mountains across the inlet. Make a long weekend of it: the Expo on Friday, the Kids Run round Lost Lagoon on Saturday, the race on Sunday, and a city that treats the first weekend of May as a running festival.
