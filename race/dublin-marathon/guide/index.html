Dublin Marathon

Dublin · Ireland
RoadUrbanMarathonEntries open Thu 12 Nov

Europe's fifth-biggest marathon, run on a Bank Holiday weekend through a city that turns out for it. A single loop, two real climbs, and a finish beside a church.

Quick verdict

A fast city loop with more shape than the elevation number suggests. Flat enough for a personal best, hilly enough at 35K to take it away. Entry is a November ballot and 47,000 applied for 22,500 places, so treat it as a race you plan a year for.

Course at a glance

8:45amStart
7 hrsCutoff
22,500Field

A downhill start from Leeson Street through St Stephen's Green and the Liberties, the first climb up Patrick Street to Christchurch, then over the Liffey to the north side and into Phoenix Park at 6K — Europe's largest enclosed park — past the zoo and along Chesterfield Avenue. Out at Castleknock, back in at Knockmaroon, down to Chapelizod and Kilmainham. Halfway at Dolphin's Barn, out through Crumlin and Terenure, then the Milltown Road incline and a 3K climb along Clonskeagh and Roebuck Roads to the top at 35K. Downhill on Fosters Avenue, along the Stillorgan and Merrion Roads past the RDS to finish at the Pepper Canister Church.

Where you'll feel it

📍Clonskeagh to Roebuck Road, 32–35K.

Roebuck Road — the hill Dubliners call their Heartbreak Hill — arrives at 35K at the end of a 3K drag. It is not steep, but it comes when the day is already hard, and the crowd at the top knows exactly why you look like that.

Best chance to push

From the top of Roebuck the last 7K falls and then flattens along the Stillorgan and Merrion Roads. The crowds are unbroken to the finish, and it is the fastest stretch of the course.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is a ballot in November for the following October. Save the race as Interested and we'll tell you the day it opens. Charity places are the second route if the ballot says no.
  2. Waves start from 8:45 at 20-minute intervals to 9:45, with a 7-hour cut-off, so the back of the field has a long, generous day.
  3. It runs on the Sunday of the October Bank Holiday weekend, so the city is on holiday and out on the streets. Book accommodation early — the whole country seems to come.

Race-day weather

Late October in Dublin: 8–13°C, a good chance of showers and an Atlantic breeze on the exposed stretches of Phoenix Park and the Stillorgan Road. Rarely cold enough to hurt, rarely dry all morning.

What runners wear

Shorts and a vest or a thin long sleeve, and a bin-bag or old top for the corral. Gloves are a common regret either way.

Typical finish times

Course records are 2:06:52 for the men (2023) and 2:24:13 for the women (2024). Around 18,500 finished in 2025, most between 3:30 and 5:00.

Worth the trip?

Yes — and for the atmosphere more than the course. It is the friendliest big-city marathon in Europe, on a course that rewards patience, in a city built for the weekend after.
