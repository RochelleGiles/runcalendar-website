UTMB Mont-Blanc

Chamonix-Mont-Blanc, Haute-Savoie · France
UltraMountainPTL · UTMB · TDS · ETC · CCC · MCC

One loop around the highest mountain in Western Europe — three countries, two nights, and a finish line the whole town waits at.

Quick verdict

The world championship of trail running, and the hardest start line to reach — entry is a lottery you earn your way into over seasons. One loop of Mont Blanc, two nights out. If ultra running has a bucket list, this is the top line.

Course at a glance

9,900 mClimbing
46 h 45Time limit
2,537 mHigh point

One anticlockwise loop from the centre of Chamonix: up the valley to Les Contamines by dark, over the cols into Italy and Courmayeur near halfway, Grand Col Ferret at 2,537 m — the roof of the race and the Swiss border — then Champex-Lac, Vallorcine and one last climb to La Flégère before the drop home. Three countries, one mountain, 174 km.

Where you'll feel it

📍After Champex-Lac, kilometres 130 to 170.

Not the first night's big cols — the three climbs that come after Champex-Lac, on your second night without sleep. Trient, Vallorcine, then the grind to La Flégère at 170 km — and the cutoffs tighten exactly as your pace falls apart.

Best chance to push

Honestly, nowhere — this race is about damage control. If you still have legs, the runnable miles out of Courmayeur before Grand Col Ferret are where the field moves; the front of the race breaks open there.

Three things to know

  1. You can't just enter. You need a UTMB Index from a qualifying race and Running Stones from UTMB World Series events, then a weighted lottery: pre-register in early January, drawn 22 January. Stones roll over — more stones, better odds, and many runners bank them across seasons.
  2. The UTMB is one of eight races in the week, with 10,000 runners between them. CCC (101 km) and OCC (60 km) share the finals billing and are the realistic first step — they run their own lotteries on the same January dates.
  3. The kit list is mandatory and checked: two headlamps, waterproof jacket and overpants, warm gloves, survival blanket, a litre of water. Warm in the valleys and freezing possible on the cols — the same night.

Late August in the high Alps: a warm valley evening at the 17:45 start, then temperatures that fall fast above 2,000 m in the dark. Editions have been shortened and rerouted for storms and snow. Pack for four seasons; the course crosses them.

What runners wear

Full mountain kit, not road kit: the mandatory list runs to two headlamps, waterproof shell and overpants, warm gloves and a survival blanket, and marshals check it. Poles are near-universal. Whatever the valley feels like at the start, dress for the cols.

Typical finish times

Jim Walmsley's 19:37:43 record means the best in the world run through one night; most of the field is out for two, against the 46 h 45 limit. In 2025, 1,665 of 2,492 starters finished. Here, the finish is the achievement.

Worth the trip?

As a runner it's the summit of the sport — but the lottery decides when you go, so start collecting stones and let CCC or OCC take you there first. As a spectator, go anyway: finals week turns Chamonix into the centre of the trail-running world, and the finish line is free.
