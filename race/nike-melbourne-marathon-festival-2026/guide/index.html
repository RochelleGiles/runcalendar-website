Melbourne Marathon

Melbourne, Victoria · Australia
RoadCity

Australia's biggest marathon on its flattest-ever course: a dawn start beside Rod Laver Arena, the lake, the bay and St Kilda Road, and a lap of the MCG to finish.

Quick verdict

This is the one Australian marathon that feels like a Major: 20,000 in the marathon alone for 2026, a 66,000-runner festival across the weekend, and a finish inside the MCG in front of a crowd. The 2026 course is the flattest in the race's 48 years — the organiser has cut the elevation gain by up to a fifth, removed the old 36 km climb from Birdwood Avenue to Domain Road, reversed Beach Road and taken out the false-finish diversion, so the run into the ground is straight and true. The catch is getting in: the marathon and half sold out before May through a 52,000-entry ballot, and the remaining routes are the waitlist (no guarantee), a charity partner or an official travel package.

Mass start at 6:15 on Sunday from Batman Avenue, 150 m north of Rod Laver Arena. The route runs out along St Kilda Road and the parkland to Albert Park Lake, then to the bay for the St Kilda foreshore and Beach Road — run the reverse way in 2026 — before turning for home past Flinders Street Station and the Botanic Gardens edge and a continuous approach to the MCG, where the last 400 m is a lap of the arena. Kilometre markers throughout; the half marathon starts at 9:45 on the same finish.

Where you'll feel it

📍There isn't one any more. The 2026 redesign removed the Birdwood Avenue–Domain Road climb at 36 km; the course now has no hill worth naming.

Less than it used to. The Birdwood Avenue climb that met tired legs at 36 km is gone, so the 2026 course has no real hill; what remains is the bay. Beach Road and the foreshore are open to whatever the wind is doing off Port Phillip, and on a blustery October morning that stretch, roughly 20–30 km, is where the pace goes. The last kilometres are flat but long: pace the middle so the MCG lap is a celebration, not a crawl.

Best chance to push

The run for home after the bay: from the foreshore back past Flinders Street Station to the MCG is flat, wide and straight, with a new continuous approach in place of the old Southbank diversion. If the wind was behind you on Beach Road, this is where a negative split lives.

Three things to know

  1. Entries are gone: the marathon and half sold out before May through the ballot. The waitlist carries no guarantee, but the charity partners and the official travel partners both offer guaranteed entry — the honest route in if you missed the ballot.
  2. Cut-offs are firm because the roads reopen: the course closes at 2 pm, and anyone slower than 5 h 30 finishes on six laps of the 1.3 km Brunton Avenue loop rather than inside the ground. Sub-4:30 on gun time gets you into the MCG before the half marathon's winner.
  3. Make a weekend of it: the 10 km (7:00) and 5 km (8:30) run on the Saturday, so a supporter can race too. Trams and trains to Richmond and Jolimont put the start and finish on foot, and the 6:15 start means an alarm before 5.

October in Melbourne is spring, and Melbourne spring means anything: typically 8–11°C at the 6:15 start, warming to the high teens by the time the back of the field finishes. Rain, sun and a gusting bay wind can all turn up in one morning; the wind on Beach Road is the part that decides how the second half feels.

What runners wear

A throwaway layer or arm sleeves for the cool start, a singlet or light tee underneath, and a cap and sunglasses for the exposed bay stretch. Racing flats are the right call on the flattest course the race has ever run.

Typical finish times

The course records are 2:09:12 (Timothy Kiplagat Ronoh, 2022) and 2:25:19 (Sinead Diver, 2018). Jack Rayner won for the second year running in 2025 in 2:15:02, and Caitlin Adams took the women's race on debut in 2:30:26. Behind them 14,500 finished in 2025; 2026's 20,000-strong field on a flatter course will bring the middle of the pack home in a hurry.

Worth the trip?

If you want the big-city marathon feeling without leaving Australia, this is it — the field, the crowd and the stadium finish are as close to a Major as the country gets, and the 2026 course is built for a time. Enter the ballot the day it opens next year; October is Melbourne at its best when the wind behaves.
