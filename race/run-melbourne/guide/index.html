Run Melbourne

Melbourne, Victoria · Australia
RoadCityHalf Marathon · 10K · 5.5KEntries open

Melbourne's big winter morning: a half, a 10K and a 5.5K on closed city streets, the Yarra, Docklands and the Tan, finishing inside Melbourne Park.

Quick verdict

Melbourne's biggest winter run: about 28,000 people across a half marathon, a 10K and a 5.5K, all on closed roads through the middle of the city, all finishing inside Melbourne Park. The half leaves in the dark at 6:30 and takes in the river, Docklands and the Tan; the 10K and the 5.5K start later and keep to the Tan and the Yarra. Flat but for one hill, and it is the one every Melbourne runner knows.

Course at a glance

6:30Half starts
~95 mClimbing
km 14–18The Tan

Every distance starts on Batman Avenue beside the river and finishes inside Melbourne Park over Tanderrum Bridge. The half heads east along Flinders Street to Fitzroy Gardens, climbs briefly past the Treasury, then turns back through the city past Flinders Street Station and follows the Yarra out to Docklands: Crown, Marvel Stadium on Harbour Esplanade, over Webb Bridge and home along the South Wharf promenade to Alexandra Gardens with the skyline ahead. Then the Tan: up St Kilda Road past the Shrine, around Government House and down Anderson Street to the river for the last three kilometres through Birrarung Marr. The 10K skips Docklands and goes straight from Flinders Street to the Tan; the 5.5K turns at Morell Bridge on Alexandra Avenue. Tap play on the course map above and fly it before you decide.

Where you'll feel it

📍Anderson Street on the Tan, kilometre 17

The Tan. The half runs flat beside the river from Docklands for nine kilometres, then St Kilda Road rises gently from 13 km past the Shrine and keeps rising around Government House until Anderson Street, about 17 km, the hill every Melbourne runner has raced up on a Tuesday night. It is only 20 metres of climbing, but it comes after an hour of flat running on cold legs. On the 10K it arrives at 5 km, fresher but just as steep. Fly the course and pause at the Tan to see how short it really is: a hard minute, then it is downhill to the Yarra and flat to the line.

Best chance to push

From the bottom of Anderson Street the course drops to the river and runs flat along the Yarra into Birrarung Marr for the last three kilometres, with the crowd thickening towards Melbourne Park. If you held something back on the Tan, this is where it goes. The same applies to the 10K from 6 km.

Three things to know

  1. Entries stay open online until 5 pm on the Saturday, and on the day at Melbourne Park from 5:30 until half an hour before each start. Distance changes are allowed until the Thursday before; withdraw by 18 June and you keep a credit for a Sole Motive event, after that nothing comes back.
  2. Bibs are posted, or collected from a Sole Motive store from 28 June to 15 July, or at the village on the Saturday or from 5:30 on race morning. Bag drop is in the village; car parking is scarce, so take the train to Richmond or Jolimont and walk in.
  3. The half has six drink stations, PURE sports drink at 7, 16 and 18.5 km, and pacers from 1:25 to 2:20. Waves go by your expected time, with a priority start for anyone under four minutes a kilometre. You need to be past Webb Bridge at 10 km by 9:15.

Race-day weather

Midwinter: about 6°C when the half starts in the dark at 6:30, sunrise around 7:30, and 11 or 12°C by the time the 5.5K goes at 10:40. July days top out near 13.5°C and it rains on roughly one day in three, usually a drizzle rather than a downpour; the 9 am breeze is light.

What runners wear

A long sleeve or arm warmers over a singlet for the start, gloves you can tuck away, and a throwaway layer for the corral: the first hour is cold and the river is open to the wind. The Tan brings you up to temperature. Road shoes; there is nothing underfoot but tarmac and bridge decking.

Worth the trip?

Yes, if you are in Victoria or want a winter half with a city centre to itself: closed roads through the landmarks, the Tan at the business end and a finish inside Melbourne Park. It is a home-town race rather than a destination one, so come for the morning, not the clock, and bring a jumper for the village.
