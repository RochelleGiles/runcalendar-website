Big Sur Marathon

Big Sur, California · United States
RoadCoastal

Twenty-six miles of closed Highway One, a grand piano at Bixby Bridge, and the Pacific for company the whole way north.

Quick verdict

The most beautiful closed road in American running, and it makes you earn every mile of it. The men's record has stood since 1987 — that tells you what the course thinks of your PB. Come for the coastline, not the clock.

Course at a glance

+2,182 ftClimbing
6 hrsCutoff
4,500Field

Point-to-point north from Big Sur Station to Carmel, every step on closed Highway One. Redwoods for the first five miles, then the road opens onto cliffs above the Pacific. Hurricane Point rises at mile 10, Bixby Bridge and its grand piano mark halfway, and the rollers keep coming until the drop to Rio Road. You start at 356 ft and finish at 10.

Where you'll feel it

📍Hurricane Point, miles 10 to 12.

Two miles and 560 feet, straight into the prevailing wind — the name is honest. You climb out of the redwoods onto open cliff and the ocean gusts find you exactly when the grade does. Bank nothing early; the piano waits at the bottom of the far side.

Best chance to push

The last five miles into Carmel trend downhill and the wind swings behind you more often than not. If Hurricane Point left anything in your legs, the run to Rio Road gives it back.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is a random drawing, not first-come: it opens 19 August and stays open two weeks, so there is no refresh race. About 4,500 marathon places, $325 if drawn. Guaranteed-entry routes exist (charity benefactor, tour packages) if you miss.
  2. The buses are not optional — the highway is closed, and they are the only way in. Loading runs 3:30–4:00 a.m., the last bus leaves at 4:30, and you wait in the dark at Big Sur Station for a 6:45 start. Bring throwaway warmth; everyone shivers together.
  3. The 6-hour limit is enforced by the road, not the organiser: checkpoints at mile 15.2 (10:30 a.m.) and 21.2 (11:50 a.m.), and Highway One reopens to traffic at 1 p.m. Train the climbs and the cutoff never enters your head.

Race-day weather

50–60°F with morning fog that usually burns off by the time you're on the cliffs. The microclimates are real: redwood shade, exposed headland wind, then Carmel sun — you can meet all three before 10 a.m.

What runners wear

Layers you can lose: it's cold in the 4 a.m. bus queue and colder waiting at Big Sur Station. Throwaway warmth for the start, singlet weather by Bixby, and a light wind layer that earns its keep on Hurricane Point.

Typical finish times

Winners run around 2:20 on a good day — the 2:16:39 course record is nearly forty years old. The big pack comes home between 4:15 and 5:00, and the 6-hour limit sweeps the road behind them. Leave your flat-course pace chart at home.

Worth the trip?

Yes, once — this is the American marathon where the course itself is the medal. The drawing, the 3:30 a.m. bus and Hurricane Point all tell you the same thing: don't bring a time goal. If you want to race, run a flat one in December; if you want to remember, run this.
