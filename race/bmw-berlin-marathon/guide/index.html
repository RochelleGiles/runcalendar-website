Berlin Marathon

The fastest marathon on earth — a runner's guide to the course where world records live.

Quick verdict

If you want to find out how fast you really are, this is the race. Dead flat, wide roads, cool air, and a finish through the Brandenburg Gate. Berlin is not a tour — it is a time trial with 55,000 friends.

Course at a glance

42mClimbing
FlatProfile
6:15Cutoff

One big loop of the city from the Tiergarten and back: past the Victory Column, out through Alexanderplatz and Potsdamer Platz, down the Kurfürstendamm, through Gendarmenmarkt — and the finish under the Brandenburg Gate. Barely 42 metres of climbing all day; more marathon world records have fallen here than on any other course.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Ku'damm stretch, kilometres 30–38

Nothing steep — just long, straight and quiet enough to hear your own doubts. Berlin races are won and lost on this boulevard: hold your rhythm and the Gate will come to you.

Best chance to push

From 30k, if the first 30 were honest. The last stretch to the Gate is where negative splits are made famous.

Three things to know

  1. The course is so flat that pacing discipline IS the race — go out 5–8 seconds per k slower than goal pace for the first 10k and cash it in late.
  2. Late September in Berlin is usually 10–18°C and dry — near-perfect marathon weather, which is half the reason the records fall here.
  3. Entry is by lottery (plus qualifying-time and tour-operator routes). The ballot for next year opens right after race week — set the date alert.

Race-day weather

10–18°CCool + dry
9:15amStart
55,000Runners

Typically 10–18°C, low humidity, 8:45am start. Cool, dry, and built for fast times — bring a throwaway layer for the corrals.

What runners wear

Short sleeves once you're moving; arm warmers that come off around 10–15k are the Berlin classic. Carbon plates everywhere — this is the one course where they get full value.

Typical finish times

Winners run close to two hours flat — this is where 2:01 happened. The big pack comes home between 3:30 and 4:30, with a firm 6:15 cutoff keeping the day sharp.

Worth the trip?

For the runner chasing a time, more than any race on earth: yes. Flat, fast, flawlessly organised, and a finish line woven into history — collecting the majors starts easiest here.
