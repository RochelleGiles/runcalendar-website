SPAR Women's Challenge Jozi

Johannesburg, Gauteng · South Africa
RoadUrban10km · 5km

The Grand Prix finale at Marks Park — 15,000 women, spring jacarandas, and the fastest women in South Africa deciding it all.

Quick verdict

The big one. The series finale packs 15,000 women into Marks Park and usually settles the Grand Prix itself — 2026's title is still open going in — with the country's best up front and half of Joburg behind them. At 1,750 metres it keeps every runner honest. Respect the altitude and the morning is unbeatable.

Course at a glance

1,750mAltitude
15,000Field cap
08:00Starts

Out of Marks Park Sports Club on Judith Road and into Emmarentia's tree-lined streets. The loop belongs to Joburg's leafy north-west — rolling rather than flat, generous with shade, and lined in early-October purple when the jacarandas oblige — before it hands you back to the club grounds and a finish-line festival that has been going since dawn.

Where you'll feel it

📍The altitude — 1,750 metres of it, from the gun to the line.

Joburg sits higher than any race most visitors have run. The air is thinner, the effort arrives earlier, and a pace that feels easy at the coast reads a gear harder here. Locals won't notice; if you're travelling up, give yourself two easy days and start conservatively.

Best chance to push

The final run back into Marks Park. Twelve thousand people packed the club in 2025, and the noise carries you the last kilometre — whatever the altitude has taken by then, the crowd gives some of it back.

Three things to know

  1. It decides the Grand Prix. The series title has a habit of being settled here — Glenrose Xaba sealed her 2025 clean sweep on this course — so the front of the race is genuinely national-class sport, on the same roads, at the same time, as everyone else's Sunday run.
  2. Early October is jacaranda season. Emmarentia and the suburbs around the route turn purple for a few weeks a year, and the race lands squarely inside them — the prettiest backdrop on the series calendar.
  3. Pre-entries close in mid-September — or the moment the cap fills, and 2025 was a capacity year. Enter when you decide, not when you remember.

Race-day weather

Highveld springSeason

Highveld spring: a crisp, clear start that warms quickly, with the sun carrying real bite by mid-morning. Storm season is beginning, but Joburg's storms are afternoon business — race morning is almost always the blue-sky half of the day.

What runners wear

A vest from the gun — the chill burns off before the first kilometre does. Cap and sunscreen matter more than layers at this altitude, where the sun works harder than the thermometer suggests. Nothing to discard; you won't need it.

Typical finish times

Glenrose Xaba won 2025 in 33:33 with the podium inside 34:35 — world-class running at altitude. The mass field is another race entirely: walkers, prams and first-timers welcome, and an hour and a half is as respectable as forty minutes.

Worth the trip?

For the series faithful, this is the one to travel for — the finale, the biggest crowd, the season's verdict. For an overseas visitor it's a yes-if: come if Joburg is already on your route, and let the altitude shape your goals, not your memory of the morning.
