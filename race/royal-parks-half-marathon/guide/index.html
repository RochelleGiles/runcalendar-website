Royal Parks Half

London, England · United Kingdom
RoadParklandHalf Marathon

London's autumn half — four Royal Parks, past the palace, on closed roads with 16,000 others and a wooden medal at the end.

Quick verdict

The prettiest half marathon in London and one of the flattest, with under 100 metres of climbing all day. A fast course dressed up as a sightseeing tour. Places go in a January ballot and sell out every year, so the way in for most people is a charity place — which is how the race was built.

Course at a glance

8:45amStart
4 hrsCutoff
< 100mClimbing

Starts on South Carriage Drive in Hyde Park and leaves the park at Hyde Park Corner, through Green Park and past Buckingham Palace, down the Mall to Admiralty Arch and Trafalgar Square, along Whitehall to the Houses of Parliament and back along Birdcage Walk and St James's Park. Constitution Hill returns you to Hyde Park, then west into Kensington Gardens past the Albert Memorial and the Royal Albert Hall before the closing loops of Hyde Park itself, along the Serpentine to the finish in the park.

Where you'll feel it

📍There isn't one — Constitution Hill, around 6 miles, is the closest thing to a rise.

The parks. After the landmarks are behind you at about 8 miles, the last five are park paths and loops of Hyde Park — wide, quiet and dead flat, with nothing to pull you along but the runners around you. It is where the day is won or drifts.

Best chance to push

The whole second half. Once you leave Kensington Gore for the park loops it is flat to the line, and the finish straight along the Serpentine is long enough to see coming.

Three things to know

  1. It sold out through a ballot in January. The organiser's own advice is to run for a charity, and there is a waiting list which in past years has cleared most of the queue. Save it as Interested and we'll tell you when the 2027 ballot opens.
  2. Waves start from 8:45 with the last away around 9:45, and the cut-off is a generous four hours after the last wave. Headphones are allowed — unusual for a London road race.
  3. There is no finisher T-shirt if you don't want one: drop it and the race plants aquatic plants along the Serpentine instead. The medal is wood, not metal.

Race-day weather

WoodMedal

Second week of October in London: 10–15°C, usually still, often grey, with the plane trees just turning. The parks give some shelter; the Mall and Whitehall give none.

What runners wear

Shorts and a vest, with a light long sleeve for the pen. It is rarely cold enough for more once you're moving.

Typical finish times

Course records are 1:05:12 for the men (2024) and 1:13:57 for the women (2020). The 2025 winners ran 1:09:53 and 1:16:48. Most of the 16,000 finish between 1:50 and 2:30.

Worth the trip?

For a first half marathon or a fast one, yes. It is London's landmarks without the marathon's scale or its ballot odds, and the money it raises is the point: over £80 million for more than 1,900 charities since 2008.
