Manchester Marathon

Manchester, England · United Kingdom
RelayCityMarathonSold out

Britain's flattest big marathon and its loudest — 54 m of climbing from Old Trafford to Oxford Road, with 150,000 people out on the streets.

Quick verdict

The UK's second-largest marathon and its flattest: 54 m of climbing in 26.2 miles, a loop out through Trafford's towns and back to a city-centre finish on Oxford Road. 42,000 ran in 2026 and 2027 sold out inside ten days. If you missed it, charity places remain and 2028 entries open the day after this race, finishers first. Save it as Interested and we'll tell you the morning it opens.

Course at a glance

54mClimbing
6 hrsCutoff
42,000+Runners

Starts on Bridgewater Way beside Old Trafford and runs out through Stretford and Sale to Altrincham town centre at miles 7–8 — the loudest stretch on the course — then Timperley and Brooklands, halfway near Sale Moor, and the quiet far turn through Baguley and Northenden at miles 16–17. Home through Chorlton, the one rise of the day at mile 21, Hulme, and the Oxford Road corridor past the university to the finish outside Whitworth Hall.

Where you'll feel it

📍Mile 21, the one rise on the route.

Miles 14–17, Sale Moor to Northenden. The crowds thin, the roads open out, and it is the furthest you will be from the finish on a course with nothing to hide behind. Then mile 21 brings the only gradient on the route, short and mild, at exactly the wrong moment. Chorlton's crowds are the reward on the other side.

Best chance to push

Miles 22–26 through Hulme and up the Oxford Road corridor: flat, straight and the noisiest four miles in British marathon running. If you held something back at Northenden, this is where you spend it.

Three things to know

  1. 2027 is at capacity — more than 40,000 public places went in the ten-day launch window last April. Charity places are still open at around £500 minimum sponsorship, and 2028 entries open the morning after this race, 2027 finishers first, then the priority list, then everyone. Save it as Interested and we'll tell you the morning it opens.
  2. Waves go from 9:00 to about 11:30 by predicted time, with a six-hour limit from your own start. There is no expo: UK entrants get their pack in the post, so check the address you registered with.
  3. It is a week before London and takes the same spring training block — and if you win the London ballot after entering Manchester, the organisers refund you. Tram to the start, walk from the finish: Old Trafford and Oxford Road are both on the Metrolink.

Race-day weather

Mid-April in Manchester: 5°C overnight, 12–13°C by the afternoon, and rain on roughly half of April days. Plan for a cool, damp start and a mild finish; the exposed miles around Timperley and Brooklands are where the wind lives.

What runners wear

Shorts and a short sleeve or vest with a bin-bag or old hoodie for the corral, gloves for the first five miles, and a cap if the forecast says rain. A dry layer in the bag drop for the walk from Oxford Road.

Typical finish times

Course records are 2:10:46 (Jonny Mellor, 2022) and 2:31:27 (Naomi Mitchell, 2023); the 2026 race was won in 2:15:19 and 2:36:56. More than 42,000 ran in 2026, and the six-hour limit sees most of them home.

Worth the trip?

For a British runner chasing a personal best, this is the course — flatter than London, bigger than everything else, and the crowds mean it. For visitors it is a friendly northern city with a tram to the start and a marathon that feels like the whole region turned out. Yes.
