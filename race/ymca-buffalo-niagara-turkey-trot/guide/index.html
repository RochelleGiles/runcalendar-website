Buffalo Turkey Trot

Buffalo, New York · United States
RoadUrban8KEntries open

The world’s oldest consecutively run footrace: five flat miles around downtown Buffalo on Thanksgiving morning, 14,000 strong, costumes welcome and a party indoors at the finish.

Quick verdict

Not a race you train for, a race you turn up to. Run every Thanksgiving since 1896, it is five flat miles around downtown Buffalo with 14,000 people, a costume contest before the gun and a party indoors after it. Race it or walk it dressed as dinner; both count.

Course at a glance

1896First run
9:00 amStart
NoneWater stops

One loop, starting and finishing beside the Convention Center. You head north up Franklin Street from Chippewa, shift across to Linwood Avenue at North Street and pass mile 1 among the old houses. Linwood runs out at Lafayette Avenue just after mile 2; a left takes you to Gates Circle and the turn for home. From there it is Delaware Avenue all the way, through miles 3 and 4, round Niagara Square, then left onto Church Street and left again onto Franklin to finish.

Where you'll feel it

📍The first mile up Franklin Street.

There is no hill to name. What slows you is 14,000 people on a downtown street: walkers, strollers at the back, costumes built for photographs rather than speed. Overall prizes go on gun time, so if you mean to race, find your pace flag early. Everyone else can relax until Linwood opens up.

Best chance to push

Delaware Avenue from Gates Circle. Nearly two and a half miles, wide and close to dead straight, past the mansions of Millionaires’ Row to Niagara Square and City Hall. If you held back on Linwood, this is where the race is.

Three things to know

  1. There is no water on the course. None. It is at the finish line and inside the Convention Center only, so drink before you line up. On a cold morning that is easier to forget than it sounds.
  2. Collect your bib before Thursday if you can: pick-up runs on the three days before, at the Independent Health Family YMCA in Amherst. Race-morning pick-up is upstairs at the Convention Center, closes 45 minutes before the start, and sits behind a security check at the Franklin Street doors.
  3. Park at the KeyBank Center lots and ride the shuttle: it runs to the Convention Center from 6:30 to 8:30 and back from 10:00 to 11:45. Be there an hour early. No dogs, bikes or wagons, and strollers start at the back. Costume contest entries close at 7:50.

Race-day weather

14,000Field

Late November beside Lake Erie: about 2°C at the start in a typical year, though the last twenty have run from −8°C to 11°C. Expect a breeze off the lake, light rain about one year in four, and now and then real snow. The Convention Center is open and warm before and after.

What runners wear

Gloves, a hat and a long-sleeved layer you can run in, with a windproof shell if the lake is blowing. Or a turkey suit, a pilgrim hat or a tutu; nobody will look twice. Leave a dry top with whoever is meeting you: the party is indoors, but the walk back to the car is not.

Typical finish times

The front of the field is properly quick: the men’s winner runs about 24 and a half minutes, the first woman about 27. Behind them the clock stops mattering. Joggers, families and walkers fill the avenue, and a bus follows the last of them in. No cut-off is published.

Worth the trip?

If you are in western New York for Thanksgiving, without a second thought. To fly in for five miles, only if the history pulls you: it first ran in 1896, a year before the Boston Marathon, and has kept its streak ever since. Its 14,000 places fill most years. Too far? Nearly every American town has a trot of its own that morning.
