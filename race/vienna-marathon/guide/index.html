Vienna City Marathon

Vienna · Austria
RoadCityMarathon · Half Marathon · 10K · 5KSold out

Austria's biggest sporting day: over the Danube on the Reichsbrücke, round the Ring, out to Schönbrunn and back through the Prater to a finish in front of the Burgtheater.

Quick verdict

Austria's biggest sporting day, and a course that reads like a city tour: over the Danube on the Reichsbrücke, into the Prater, round the Ring past the Opera, out the Wienzeile to Schönbrunn and back down Mariahilfer Straße, with a finish on the Ring in front of the Burgtheater. Most of the field runs the half, which gets all of that and turns for home at 20 km; the marathon goes on along the Donaukanal and down the long straight of the Prater Hauptallee before the Ring again. Flat but for the bridge, with a women's course record of 2:20:06 set in 2026. It fills by September the year before, and a warm April can make the second half honest work.

Course at a glance

Everyone starts together on Wagramer Straße beside UNO City and crosses the Reichsbrücke over the Danube, the one real rise of the day, then runs Lassallestraße to Praterstern and into the Prater for a first taste of the Hauptallee to about 5 km. Schüttelstraße along the Donaukanal brings you to the Ring at 9 km: the Stubenring, the Kärntner Ring past the Opera at 10, then the Linke Wienzeile beside the Naschmarkt all the way out to Schönbrunn at 15. Mariahilfer Straße carries you back towards the centre from 18 to 20, and at 20.3 km the field divides under two arches: the half turns right through the red one into Bellariastraße and finishes within the kilometre, the marathon goes straight on through the blue one. From there it is Universitätsstraße and Alsergrund to the Donaukanal at Roßauer Lände, 23 to 26 km along the water, Praterstern again and the Prater Hauptallee from 27 to 35 km, out past the stadium to the Lusthaus and back. Rustenschacherallee, the Franzensbrücke at 38 and the Stubenring bring you round to the Kärntner Ring at 41 and the finish straight on the Burgring, with the Rathausplatz as the medal plaza. The relay changes at Schönbrunn, Roßauer Lände and the stadium.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Prater Hauptallee, out and back, kilometres 27–35

The Prater Hauptallee the second time, 27 to 35 km. It is dead flat and dead straight, four kilometres of chestnut avenue out to the Lusthaus and the same four back, with the crowds thinner than anywhere on the Ring and nothing to break the line of sight. In 2026 the men's race was decided here at 32 km, and so is most people's day. Before that, the split at 20 km is its own test: you watch the half turn right for a finish 800 metres away while you go straight on with 22 to run, and on the warm day in 2026 a good number of marathoners took the red arch instead. Decide before the start what you will do at that gantry, then do it.

Best chance to push

The Ring. The first time, 9 to 11 km, is where the pace settles on the widest, smoothest road in the city with the Opera going past; the second time, from the Stubenring at 39.5 km round to the Burgtheater, is the loudest kilometre and a half of the race. Nothing goes uphill after the Reichsbrücke, so if the Hauptallee did not take it from you, the Kärntner Ring at 41 is where the negative split is spent.

Three things to know

  1. Entries open on the day of the race for the following year and close when full: for this edition the half closed in early September, the marathon on 20 September, the relay two days later. Waiting lists are free to join and places that come back are handed on until 7 March. The two doors still open are the Sunday 10K, 3,000 places from the same start at 8:00, and the Saturday-evening 5K round the Ring, 5,000 places from Schottentor at 18:15.
  2. Your start block depends on paperwork: a result of 3:35 or faster for the marathon, 1:40 for the half, from the last three seasons, uploaded by 12 March, or you start from the rear blocks. Bag drop has to be booked by the same date; bags go in at Vienna Sports World in the Marx Halle on the Friday or Saturday, or at the start between 7:00 and 8:30, and come back at the Rathaus until 16:30. Bibs come only from the Marx Halle, Friday 9:00 to 19:00 and Saturday 9:00 to 18:00; nothing is handed out on Sunday.
  3. The finish on the Ring closes at 13:00 for the half and 15:30 for the marathon and relay. Pacers run every quarter hour from 3:00 to 4:45, fifteen water points line the route with Powerade and bananas at every third one, and the official splits come every 5 km. The weekend's other fixture is the Konzert zum Marathon in the Musikverein's Golden Hall on the Saturday morning, which sells out as the race does.

Race-day weather

Mid-April in Vienna is a cool start and a mild finish: around 6 to 8°C on the Reichsbrücke at 9:00, the low teens on the Ring by noon and up to 16°C on an ordinary afternoon. Rain falls on roughly one April day in three, usually showers. The variable is a warm spring: 2026 was good weather by the organiser's account and still warm enough to send marathoners through the half's arch and cut the marathon's finishers to around 8,700 from 9,300 the year before.

What runners wear

Short sleeves or a singlet and shorts, with arm warmers for the bridge and a throwaway layer for the corrals on Wagramer Straße, which are open and exposed to the river before nine. Sunglasses and a cap earn their place on the Hauptallee, where the avenue gives shade only on one side, and racing flats are the right call: the surface is good asphalt from the bridge to the Burgtheater.

Typical finish times

The course records are 2:05:08 and 2:20:06, the women's set in 2026, and the winners usually come home around 2:07 and 2:21. The half brought home about 13,200 finishers in 2026 and the marathon about 8,700, with the course open until 13:00 and 15:30 respectively; a 10K of more than 2,000 ran the same start at 8:00. The Austrian half record on this course is 1:02:58.

Worth the trip?

Yes, for the city as much as the course: the Ring twice, Schönbrunn, the Prater and a finish in front of the Burgtheater, on a route that is flat from the bridge onward, with a concert in the Musikverein the morning before. Run the half if you want the sights without the Hauptallee; run the marathon for the Donaukanal and the Prater at full stretch. Either way enter the day entries open, because both are gone by September.
