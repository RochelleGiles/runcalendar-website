Run Fest Silverstone

Silverstone, England · United Kingdom
RoadCountrysideHalf Marathon · 10K · 5K · Family MileSold out

A half, 10K, 5K and family mile at the home of the British Grand Prix: down the Hangar Straight and home in view of the Copse grandstand.

Quick verdict

The closest most of us will get to the British Grand Prix: four races on the Silverstone circuit and its service roads, flat and UK Athletics-licensed, with pacers from 1:25 to 3:00 for the half. General entries have sold out; charity places are the way in.

Course at a glance

770 mHangar Straight
10:00Start
4 hTime limit

Everyone but the family mile starts together at ten, in nine pace pens, and finishes in the same place, in view of the Copse grandstand and the pit lane gantry. The 5K is an out-and-back on the track itself. The 10K and half mix stretches of the Grand Prix circuit, the Hangar Straight among them, with the circuit's service roads and infield.

Where you'll feel it

📍The infield and service roads, away from the grandstands.

There are no hills. The test is the open middle miles on a former airfield, away from the grandstands and under whatever November brings. The half has four water stations and the 10K two; the 5K has none, so carry a bottle if you want one.

Best chance to push

The Hangar Straight: 770 metres, the longest on the circuit and dead flat. It is the one place on the course to wind up and see how fast you feel, on the same tarmac as the Grand Prix.

Three things to know

  1. General entries have sold out, so a charity place is the way in: pick a partner charity, agree its fundraising commitment and it registers you. Entry transfers and deferrals close a few weeks before race day, and swapping a bib earns a DNF.
  2. Car parks open at seven and the race village, in the National Paddock, at half past; park by eight, because every car park is about a mile's walk away, over two bridges. Parking is free. By train, go to Milton Keynes Central for the coach, and expect crowded buses.
  3. In-ear and over-ear headphones are banned; bone-conduction sets are the only exception. Bag drop works on a numbered wristband, the changing garages are not private and there are no showers. Every finisher gets a medal and a buff, and spectators register for free.

Race-day weather

November in Northamptonshire: highs around 10°C and lows of 4°C, so expect single figures at the ten o'clock start. It rains on more than one day in three, there are about two hours of sunshine a day, and the odd morning brings frost.

What runners wear

A throwaway layer for the wait in the pens: clothes left at the start go to Age UK Northamptonshire. Gloves and a long-sleeved top for the race itself if it's cold, and a cap if it's raining.

Typical finish times

Pacers cover 1:25 to 3:00 for the half, 40 to 60 minutes for the 10K and 20 to 35 for the 5K, and every distance has four hours. At the first running the half was won in 70:41 and 84:59.

Worth the trip?

Yes, if the Grand Prix is why you're here: you run on the circuit itself, on a flat, licensed course with pacers. It is a long morning, with a mile's walk each way from the car park and single-figure temperatures; plan for both and it is a memorable one.
