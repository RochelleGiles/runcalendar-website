Valencia Marathon

València, Valencia · Spain
RoadCity42K · 21KSold out

The world's fastest big-city marathon — 62 m of climbing, a December morning by the sea, and a finish across the water at the City of Arts and Sciences.

Quick verdict

Where the marathon goes to run fast: 2:01:48 and 2:14:00 are the course records, and 30,669 finished in 2025, famously thousands of them under three hours. A flat loop at sea level, cool December air, and the blue carpet across the water at the finish. 2026 is full; 2027 entry is a free ballot in the fortnight after race day. Course at a glance

62mClimbing
5:30Cutoff
30,600+Finishers

Starts at the Plaça de la Marató beside the City of Arts and Sciences and heads for the sea — past the Veles e Vents building and the Marina in the first 5K — then the Malvarrosa seafront before turning back into the city along the Avenida del Puerto. The second half is the tour: the old town past the Plaza del Ayuntamiento and the medieval Torres de Serranos, the wide avenues along the Turia gardens, and the run home to the City of Arts and Sciences, where the last hundred metres are a blue carpet laid across the water. Aid every 5K to 30K, then every 2–3K.

Where you'll feel it

📍None worth naming — 62 m of climbing in the whole race.

The wide avenues between 25 and 35K. They are the fastest part of the course and the most exposed — the one place the sea breeze can find you — and they come after the old town's narrow streets have broken your rhythm. The crowd thins for a few kilometres here. Everyone who blows up at Valencia blows up here.

Best chance to push

The last 7K back to the City of Arts and Sciences: wide, straight, lined ten deep and running very slightly downhill to the water. If the exposed avenues have not taken you, this is where the negative split happens.

Three things to know

  1. Entry for 2027 is a free ballot: for 2026 it ran 16–26 December with the draw on 12 January and 36,000 bibs, and 2025 finishers got a four-day loyalty window first. Expect the same fortnight after this year's race. Save it as Interested and we'll tell you the morning it opens.
  2. Nine waves from 8:15 to 9:35 by predicted finish, a 5:30 limit and a hard cut at 25K at 12:57 — this is a fast race that runs to a fast clock.
  3. Aid stations are every 5K to 30K and then every 2–3K: plan your gels on that rhythm. And plan the weekend — the half runs the same city in October, and December in Valencia is 15°C and sunny more often than not.

Race-day weather

Early December in Valencia: around 6–8°C at the 8:15 start, 14–16°C by midday, usually dry and often cloudless. The seafront kilometres can carry a breeze. It is the coolest morning of the Spanish racing year, which is the point.

What runners wear

Singlet and shorts with arm warmers or a thin long sleeve for the first 10K along the sea, and a throwaway layer for the corral — the start is in shade and the air is cold until the sun clears the buildings. Gloves optional, sunglasses for the run home.

Typical finish times

Course records are 2:01:48 (Sisay Lemma, 2023) and 2:14:00 (Joyciline Jepkosgei, 2025). 30,669 finished in 2025, and the field is famously front-loaded — thousands under three hours — but the road stays open 5:30 and the finish is loud for every one of them.

Worth the trip?

Yes — this is the one to fly for. A personal-best course by every measure, a December weekend by the Mediterranean, and a finish line that people travel to run across. Book the beds the day the ballot says yes; the city fills.
