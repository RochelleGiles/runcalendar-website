Tromsø Polar Night Marathon

Tromsø, Troms · Norway
RoadFjordsEntries open

A marathon run where the sun does not rise at all — by headtorch, on ice, under whatever the sky is doing.

Quick verdict

The sun has not been above the horizon in Tromsø for six weeks and will not rise on race day either. You start at one in the afternoon in the last of the blue twilight and finish in full darkness. Bring studs, because the organiser will not promise you asphalt.

Course at a glance

1,220mBridge
5 hrsCutoff
2 h 25Halfway gate

Point-to-point from Sjøtun on the outer coast of Kvaløya island, along the shore road, then over the 1,220-metre Sandnessund Bridge onto Tromsøya and down Storgata to finish by the town hall. The route is marked with torches, weather permitting.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Sandnessund Bridge, in the last quarter.

The Kvaløya coast road: no streetlights, no city, an exposed shoreline and whatever comes off the water, for a very long time inside a headtorch beam. Then a 1,220-metre bridge ramp with 41 metres of clearance and nowhere to hide from the crosswind.

Best chance to push

Off the bridge and into Tromsø. You come out of the dark island into a lit city, torches along the street and people out in the cold. The last kilometres down Storgata are the whole reason to be here.

Three things to know

  1. A headtorch with a charged battery and a reflective vest are mandatory kit for the marathon — not advice. The half, 10 km and 5 km require none, but all three start after twilight ends at 14:30, so carry a light whatever you enter.
  2. The organiser refuses to promise a surface. Their own guidance covers three scenarios — ice, dry snow, dry asphalt — and you will not know which until a few days out. Pack for two of them.
  3. There is a halfway gate, not just a finish cutoff. You must reach 20 km within 2 hours 25, the overall limit is five hours from the 13:00 gun, and the finish closes at 18:00. On an icy year that is tight.

Race-day weather

NoneDaylight

January in Tromsø runs from a few degrees above zero to double figures below. There is no daylight at all — civil twilight from 09:12 to 14:30 is the whole of it. The aurora is plausible rather than promised; it depends entirely on cloud.

What runners wear

Layers you can still run in at minus ten, and a windproof shell for the bridge. Studded shoes if the forecast says ice. Cover hands and face: the wind off the strait is what hurts, not the air temperature.

Typical finish times

The course records are 2:47:45 and 2:55:38, both set in 2026 — slow by road standards, which tells you what the conditions do. In 2026 the men's half winner ran 1:19:34, eleven minutes outside the record. Five hours for the marathon, three for the half.

Worth the trip?

If running a marathon in permanent darkness above the Arctic Circle appeals, nothing else comes close. If it sounds like an ordeal, it partly is: Norway is expensive, the ice is real, and the cutoff leaves no room for a cautious run.
