Abu Dhabi Marathon

Abu Dhabi · United Arab Emirates

The Gulf's flattest big-city marathon: a six o'clock start in December, Qasr Al Hosn and the Grand Mosque on the way, and a finish on the Corniche with 37,000 runners across five distances.

Quick verdict

The biggest road race in the Gulf and one of the flattest marathons anywhere: under 100 metres of climb, a six o'clock start in mid-December and the Abu Dhabi Corniche for the finish. About 5,000 run the marathon and relay; 32,000 more run the 10K, 5K and 2.5K behind them. Pick it for a winter-sun marathon on a course you can race.

Course at a glance

A flat city loop on closed roads. The 2026 course returns to the Corniche, and the organiser had not published the turns when this was written; past editions ran from the ADNOC headquarters along the Corniche to Qasr Al Hosn, down Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Street towards the Grand Mosque and back to the waterfront, with the relay changing hands at 22 km. Expect long straights, wide boulevards and a finish by the sea. The 10K starts half an hour after the marathon; the 2.5K and 5K run in waves from 8:30.

Where you'll feel it

📍The warmth after eight o'clock, not a hill

There is no hill to feel. What you feel is the sun: the start is cool at six, the air is coastal and humid, and by eight the long inland straights are warming towards the twenties with little shade. The second half is where a pace set in the dark comes due.

Best chance to push

The last kilometres on the Corniche, where the sea breeze arrives and the crowds thicken. Everything before it is flat and fast, so the course gives nothing back later: the push is whatever heat you have kept in reserve.

Three things to know

  1. Entries are first come, first served from late July and the race has not sold out in recent years. There is no deferral and no transfer, and refunds ended on 31 August. Race packs come by courier within the UAE in early December or from the race village, never on race day.
  2. Running from Dubai? A pre-booked shuttle leaves Ibn Battuta metro at 2:45 in the morning for the six o'clock start, and the Corniche road closes for the day, so use the park-and-ride or arrive early. Bag drop opens at 4:30 for marathon, relay and 10K runners.
  3. Water and fruit roughly every three kilometres with gels at intervals, and pace groups from 3:20 to 5:00 in past years. The marathon cut-off has been 6 hours 15. Elite men have run 2:04 here; the women's record is 2:19:15.

Race-day weather

Cool at the start, warm by the finish: around 14 to 17°C at six, into the mid-twenties by ten, with coastal humidity high through the night. Rain is rare, about two days in the whole month.

What runners wear

Singlet and shorts from the gun; a throwaway layer is optional at six and a cap worth having by eight. Racing flats: it is tarmac throughout and flat.

Typical finish times

Course records 2:04:40 by Reuben Kiprop Kipyego in 2019 and 2:19:15 by Brigid Kosgei in 2023, both on earlier courses. The 2025 winners ran 2:07 and 2:21. Behind them about 5,000 ran the marathon and relay, with 32,000 more in the shorter races and runners from over 150 countries.

Worth the trip?

Yes, for a December marathon in winter sun on a flat, label-certified course in a city that closes its roads for you; Qasr Al Hosn and the Grand Mosque are on the way and the Corniche finish is the picture you take home. Dubai Marathon is five weeks later, which makes a two-race trip possible. It is not a crowd-lined European marathon: much of the course is wide, quiet boulevard.
