Mumbai Marathon

Mumbai, Maharashtra · India
RoadUrban skylineMarathon · Half Marathon · Open 10k

India's biggest morning of running — 70,000 across the day, the Sea Link at first light, Marine Drive twice, and a half marathon that most of the field actually runs.

Quick verdict

India's biggest morning of running, and Asia's richest road race: around 70,000 people across the day, a World Athletics Gold Label field at the front and the whole city out on the pavements behind them. Most of the amateur field runs the half — and the half gets the Bandra–Worli Sea Link at first light too. Flat but for one hill, Peddar Road, which the marathon meets twice and remembers at 36 km. The marathon is the one door still open; the half and the 10K filled within days.

Course at a glance

~360 mClimbing
5:00Start
7 hCut-off

The marathon leaves Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus in the dark, past Flora Fountain and out onto Marine Drive for the first of two passes along the bay. The Peddar Road climb comes at 10 km, then Haji Ali and the Coastal Road north to the Bandra–Worli Sea Link — the long, gradual rise over the water that everyone photographs and nobody enjoys at kilometre 20. Down the Mahim ramp at 24 km, through Shivaji Park and Siddhivinayak, a U-turn at Worli around 30 km, and back down the Coastal Road to Haji Ali. Peddar Road again at 36–37 km, then Chowpatty, Marine Drive for 39–41 km and the finish at Azad Maidan. The half starts at Mahim Reti Bunder and joins the same roads: the Sea Link, Peddar Road once, Marine Drive home. The 10K is Marine Drive out and back.

Where you'll feel it

📍Peddar Road, second pass, kilometres 36–37

Peddar Road the second time. It is only 25 metres of climbing, but it arrives at 36 km after eleven exposed kilometres on the Coastal Road and the Sea Link, in humidity that has been working on you since the start. Runners call it Heartbreak Hill for a reason: the ones who slow down deliberately on the way up are the ones still running on the way down. Before that, the Sea Link's rise feels longer than it is, and the 30–33 km stretch of Coastal Road is flat, featureless and lonely — hold your rhythm through it.

Best chance to push

From the top of Peddar Road at about 37.5 km the race is downhill to Chowpatty and then dead flat along Marine Drive to the finish, usually with a helpful breeze behind you. If you climbed it honestly, this is where the negative split lives — and the crowds on Marine Drive will carry you the last two kilometres.

Three things to know

  1. Humidity, not heat, is the opponent: 20°C at the start sounds mild, but at 75–80% humidity your effort feels three or four degrees warmer. Start slower than your certificate says you can.
  2. Entry is by timing certificate, not ballot — your qualifying time sets your start wave, the marathon start line closes at 5:30, and the marathon window runs to early November or until places fill.
  3. Bib collection is at the expo on the three days before the race; outstation and overseas runners get the last day. Locals hand out oranges and biscuits on Peddar Road — take them.

Mid-January is Mumbai's coolest, driest week: around 20–21°C at the 5:00 start with 75–80% humidity, still dark until sunrise at about 7:10, and climbing towards the high twenties by the time the back of the marathon reaches Marine Drive. Rain is almost unheard of.

What runners wear

A singlet and the lightest sweat-wicking kit you own — never cotton. A cap and sunglasses earn their place once the sun is up over the Coastal Road, and sunscreen goes on before the start, not after. Racing flats suit the course; there is nothing technical about it.

Typical finish times

The course records stand at 2:07:32 and 2:24:15 and the winners usually come home in 2:09–2:10 and 2:25. Behind them the marathon is a slow race by international standards: 11,600 finishers last year with the median at five hours flat and only 77 under three. The half's 12,600 finishers cluster between 2:20 and 2:40. The marathon cut-off is seven hours from the 5:30 start-line close.

Worth the trip?

Yes, if you want the biggest running day in Asia rather than the fastest: the Sea Link at dawn is one of the sport's great sights, Marine Drive is lined the whole way, and the city treats the morning as a festival. Go in with a plan for the humidity and a time goal you would set for a warm day, bring a timing certificate for entry, and remember the half and the 10K sell out within days of opening in August.
