London Marathon

The people's major — a runner's guide to the flattest, loudest lap of London.

Quick verdict

The hardest ballot in running guards the flattest course of all the majors. London is a fast, roaring, landmark-a-mile celebration — Cutty Sark, Tower Bridge, the Embankment, and a finish in front of Buckingham Palace. Worth every rejection email it takes to get here.

Course at a glance

FlatProfile

Point-to-point from Greenwich to The Mall: through maritime Greenwich past the Cutty Sark at 10k, over Tower Bridge at halfway — the defining moment — then the long Canary Wharf loop before the Thames Embankment carries you past Big Ben to Buckingham Palace. Just 127m of climbing all day; the flattest course on the majors calendar.

Where you'll feel it

📍Miles 14–21, the Isle of Dogs and Canary Wharf loop

After Tower Bridge's wall of noise, the crowds thin and the towers close in — this is where London is won, on discipline and splits rather than atmosphere. The Embankment will bring the roar back when you've earned it.

Best chance to push

From mile 22 on the Embankment — flat, straight, deafening, with Big Ben ahead and the palace waiting. The best finishing stretch in road running.

Three things to know

  1. The ballot is the hardest in running — around 1 in 13 odds from over 1.3 million applicants. Enter every year, and consider charity places if the cause moves you.
  2. If you cross halfway on Tower Bridge feeling fantastic, you probably went out too fast — the quiet Canary Wharf miles are where that mistake gets expensive.
  3. Late April in London is 7–15°C with rain always possible — perfect racing weather most years, but pack for wet and cool.

Late April: 7–15°C, often overcast, occasional rain — genuinely excellent marathon conditions most years. A light throwaway poncho covers the wet-start scenario.

What runners wear

Vest and shorts with arm warmers is the London standard; a bin-bag or old jumper for the Blackheath start. Nothing you have not raced in before — this is a PB day if you let it be.

Typical finish times

Winners run 2:01–2:04 — world records have fallen here. The great pack finishes 3:45–5:00, and London famously cheers its final finishers as loudly as its first.

Worth the trip?

Completely — it pairs the PB potential of Berlin with the occasion of New York, in one of the world's great cities. If the ballot ever says yes, drop everything.
