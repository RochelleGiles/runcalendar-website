Tokyo Marathon

Tokyo · Japan

The precision major — a runner's guide to the fastest tour of the world's biggest city.

Quick verdict

The most flawlessly run marathon on earth, in the most orderly chaos on earth. Flat, fast, immaculately organised, and threaded through neon Tokyo — from Shinjuku's towers to the temple gates of Asakusa. Hard to get into; unforgettable once you are.

Course at a glance

Net ↓38mDownhill
1Real climb
7 hrsCutoff

Point-to-point from the Shinjuku skyscrapers to Tokyo Station, with long out-and-back arms reaching Asakusa's Kaminarimon Gate, the sumo district of Ryogoku, and glittering Ginza. Net downhill overall with one real bridge climb near 28k — otherwise flat, wide and built for even splits.

Where you'll feel it

📍Kilometres 25–35, the out-and-back stretch

You can see runners miles ahead of you — and miles behind — right when the 30k wall arrives, with the course's one proper climb thrown in at 28k. Tokyo is won with patience here, not pace.

Best chance to push

From 35k, Ginza to the finish — the crowds thicken from 38k and pull you down the final boulevards to Tokyo Station.

Three things to know

  1. Getting in is the hard part — lottery odds run around 1 in 10, with over 300,000 entering. The general ballot opens in mid-August each year: apply the moment it opens, and expect to try more than once.
  2. Early March is 5–14°C and usually clear — cold in the corrals, ideal on the move. A throwaway layer at the start is essential kit.
  3. The cutoffs are strict and enforced at checkpoints along the way, not just at the line — 7 hours gun time, so start-line delays count against you.

Early March: typically 5–14°C and clear. Cold waiting, perfect running — the classic Tokyo morning starts chilly and finishes in sunshine.

What runners wear

Arm warmers or a thin long sleeve under your race kit, light gloves for the first 15k, and a disposable layer for the corral. Technical fabrics only — the temperature swing is real.

Typical finish times

Winners run low-2:02 to 2:05. The heart of the field comes home between 3:45 and 5:00, and the 7-hour gun-time cutoff is enforced to the minute.

Worth the trip?

Yes — and not only for the race. It is the most organised sporting event you will ever attend, in a city that rewards every extra day you stay. In 2027 it also hosts the Abbott World Marathon Majors Age Group World Championships, so you'll share the road with the best age-group runners in the world. Pair it with cherry-blossom season starting weeks later and make it the trip of a running lifetime.
