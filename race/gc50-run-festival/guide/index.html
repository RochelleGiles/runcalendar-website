GC50 Run Festival

Gold Coast, Queensland · Australia
UltraCoastal

Australia's 50km road championship on a dead-flat beachfront course — a runner's honest guide to racing four laps of the Oceanway.

Quick verdict

The fastest 50 in the country, and the friendliest place to run your first one. Dead flat, aid roughly every two kilometres, and a 7 hour 40 cutoff that leaves nobody behind. Come to race the clock — the nation's best will be doing the same.

Course at a glance

4 × 12.5kmLaps
Dead flatProfile
7h 40mCutoff

Four identical laps of the Oceanway from Queen Elizabeth Park, Coolangatta: north along the beachfront to the turn at Kropp Park, Tugun, and back the way you came. Closed roads and ocean views throughout, with Lap Alley pulling you through the start–finish three times before the last one counts. Seven drink stations per lap.

Where you'll feel it

📍Lap four, kilometres 37 to 50.

No hill to blame — this course gives you nothing to hide behind. By lap four the sun is high and the humidity climbing, and the flatness that made the first hour feel free means your legs never get a change of gear. Even pacing wins here.

Best chance to push

Lap three is the quiet one — the field has spread, the path is clear, the morning still cool. Build rhythm there rather than speed, and let Lap Alley's crowd carry you through the final turn.

Three things to know

  1. The gun goes at 5:00am and the start line closes five minutes later — this race starts in the dark. Sunrise comes up over the Pacific about fifteen minutes in, and it's the best moment of the day. Sort your alarm, not your excuses.
  2. You're sharing the road with the Australian 50km Championship field, and the course record is 2:56:36, set in 2025. Don't let the front pack talk you off your own plan in lap one.
  3. Drink stations carry water and Fuel X sports drink; gels only appear from 8:00am, in limited supply. If your fuelling plan is gel-heavy, carry your own — the laps make a belt or a stashed bottle easy to manage.

Race-day weather

17–25°COctober
5:00amStart
~73%Humidity

Spring on the southern Gold Coast: around 17°C in the pre-dawn start pen, mid-twenties by the time the finish closes at 12:45pm, humidity near 73 per cent. The first two laps are the cool ones — the race warms up exactly as it gets harder.

What runners wear

Singlet and shorts from the gun — 17°C at 5:00am is mild, not cold, and you'll be grateful by nine o'clock. A cap and sunscreen matter more than a throwaway layer here. Road shoes, not trail: the Oceanway is sealed path and closed road the whole way.

Typical finish times

The records stand at 2:56:36 and 3:15:46 — championship pace at the front. The 7 hour 40 cutoff is generous for the distance: a steady nine-minute kilometre gets you home with daylight to spare before the line closes at 12:45pm.

Worth the trip?

If you want your first ultra, or a fast 50 against a national-championship clock, yes — there may be no easier place in Australia to step past the marathon. If your ultras need mountains and solitude, four laps of a beachfront path will feel like a track meet. Know which runner you are.
