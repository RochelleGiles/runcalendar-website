Tarawera Ultramarathon

Rotorua, Bay of Plenty · New Zealand
TrailForestTMiler · T102 · T50 · T21 · T14Entries open

Eight lakes, sacred maunga and a pounamu at the finish — New Zealand's great trail race runs from geyser steam to the Village Green.

Quick verdict

New Zealand's flagship trail weekend and the friendliest big ultra in the southern hemisphere: five races from 14 to 163 km through Te Arawa's lakes and forests, mostly runnable, endlessly scenic. The 102 km hands out Western States Golden Tickets; the miler hands out pounamu.

Course at a glance

3,800mClimbing
36 hrsCutoff
15Aid stations

The 163 km loop leaves Te Puia's geothermal steam at 4 a.m., passes the Buried Village to a boat crossing of Lake Rotomahana, then swings beneath Mt Tarawera's three sacred peaks into Tarawera Forest and the falls. The long road home strings together Ōkataina's native bush, Blue Lake and the Redwoods before finishing on Rotorua's Village Green.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Western Ōkataina track, deep in the second half.

The native-bush single track around Lake Ōkataina is the technical heart of the course — roots, stairs and remote kilometres arriving after the runnable early miles have already emptied your legs. In a wet year the whole forest turns to mud and record attempts simply stop.

Best chance to push

The finish. From Blue Lake through the Redwoods the trails turn wide and fast, dropping past Sulphur Point's steam to the Village Green — a closing stretch that lets you actually run it in.

Three things to know

  1. Come on Friday morning: the pōwhiri at Te Puia welcomes every runner onto Te Arawa land, and each start opens with karakia. This is the race's soul, not a sideshow — and miler finishers receive a pounamu pendant, not a medal.
  2. Entry is first-come with tiered pricing — no lottery — and every distance is open now. Only the miler asks for a qualifier: an official 50 km-plus result uploaded by early January. The 102 km is a Western States Golden Ticket race; its top two earn automatic entry.
  3. The kit rule is blunt — 'no mandatory gear = no start': taped-seam jacket, hydrophobic thermals, a survival bag you can climb into, hi-vis for the road sections on the two long races. Checks are random and mid-race.

Mid-summer in Rotorua: high teens to mid-twenties with humidity as the real opponent — race days have recorded 91%. The forest shades most of the course, but a wet year runs sodden end to end; 2026 drowned every record attempt.

What runners wear

Light and airy until the sky argues — then the mandatory list earns its place. Nothing cotton, everything quick-drying, and shoes that hold wet roots: the mud is famous when it comes.

Typical finish times

Daniel Jones and Ruth Croft own the 102 km — four straight wins each and records of 7:17 and 8:24. Miler winners run 15–16 hours against the 36-hour limit, and 4,326 of 4,755 starters finished in 2026: this race wants you to make it.

Worth the trip?

Yes, and not only for the running. The pōwhiri, the karakia at dawn, the pounamu at the end — no other major event treats the land it crosses like this. Bring the family to Rotorua in high summer: the 14 and 23 km races are made for them.
