Toronto Waterfront Marathon

Toronto, Ontario · Canada
RoadLakeMarathon · Half Marathon · 5K

Canada's biggest marathon — a flat run along Lake Ontario through a dozen neighbourhoods, from Yonge Street to the steps of Old City Hall.

Quick verdict

Canada's largest marathon and its fastest big-city course: a long out-and-back along Lake Ontario with about 177 m of climbing in total, a World Athletics Elite Label field at the front and more than 7,000 finishers behind it. Entry is first-come and 2026 sold out in June. Save it as Interested and we'll tell you the morning 2027 opens.

Course at a glance

177mClimbing
6 hrsCutoff
7,300+Finishers

Starts on Yonge Street near Elm, runs north to Bloor and west to Bathurst — mind the streetcar tracks — then drops to the lake. The long westward leg along Lake Shore Boulevard passes Exhibition Place and turns at Ellis Avenue below High Park, then comes back east along the water, over the Spadina fly-over and along Queens Quay past the ferry terminal. East of downtown a hairpin on Bayview by the Don, Eastern Avenue over the Don Valley Parkway, and Queen Street East through Leslieville to the Beaches turnaround near Maclean Avenue. Home the same way to a gentle rise up Bay Street, finishing outside Old City Hall.

Where you'll feel it

📍Bay Street, the final kilometre.

The east end, 30–38K. The Beaches leg is the one stretch away from the lake breeze and the deepest crowds, with the Don Valley Parkway bridge on Eastern Avenue to climb on the way out and again on the way back — short and sharp, at exactly the point the flat kilometres stop feeling free. Then Bay Street rises 23 m in the final kilometre, a 2.3% grade you will notice.

Best chance to push

The return along the waterfront, 15–28K: flat, sheltered, the skyline ahead of you and the loudest crowds of the day at Harbourfront and Queens Quay. Bank the rhythm there and hold something back for the Beaches.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is first-come — no ballot, no waitlist, and once a distance is full it is full. 2026 sold out in June, four months before the start. Save the race as Interested and we'll tell you the morning 2027 opens.
  2. Waves go from 8:00 to 9:25, corralled by predicted finish, with a 6-hour limit on the marathon and 3:30 on the half. The 5K runs on the Saturday, so the whole family can race the same weekend.
  3. Streetcar tracks on Bathurst and Queen East and four hairpin turns: this is a fast course, not a mindless one. Look down at the crossings and run the tangents on the turns.

Mid-October in Toronto: about 10°C at the start and a typical range of 7–14°C, with the lake keeping the Lake Shore leg the coolest and breeziest part of the morning. Wet years happen; cold ones are rare this early in autumn.

What runners wear

Singlet or short sleeves and shorts, with a throwaway layer for the corral — the Yonge Street start sits in shade at 8am. Gloves for the first 5K are a common comfort, and arm warmers earn their place on the exposed lakefront.

Typical finish times

Course records are 2:05:00 (Philemon Rono, 2019) and 2:20:44 (Waganesh Mekasha, 2024), and Cam Levins ran the Canadian record of 2:09:25 here in 2018. More than 7,300 finished the marathon in 2025, the most ever in a Canadian marathon, and the road stays open a full six hours.

Worth the trip?

For Canadians, this is the one: the biggest field, the fastest city course and the national stage. For visitors, a fall weekend in a lakefront city with direct flights from almost everywhere, a flat course built for a personal best and a finish outside a Romanesque city hall. Yes.
