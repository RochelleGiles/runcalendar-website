Quick verdict

Dubai's postcard in 21 kilometres: from Coca-Cola Arena past the Museum of the Future and the Burj Khalifa, over two bridges and down the coast to a finish facing the Burj Al Arab. Over 15,000 from 172 nationalities ran the last edition, and it sold out. Pick it for the skyline at sunrise.

Course at a glance

6:30Start
3 hCut-off
2 bridgesClimbs

Point to point on closed roads. The first nine kilometres loop the Sheikh Zayed Road corridor past the Emirates Towers, the Museum of the Future and One Za'abeel, then drop into Downtown beside the Burj Khalifa. The course crosses Sheikh Zayed Road, works west to Jumeirah Street by 12 km, crosses the Dubai Canal Bridge near 14 km and runs the last seven kilometres down the coast to Sunset Beach, with the Burj Al Arab ahead.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Dubai Canal Bridge, near 14 km

The two bridges are the only climbs: Sheikh Zayed Road at about 9 km and the Dubai Canal Bridge near 14, which the organiser warns runners underestimate. Both come after the turns through Downtown have taken the rhythm out of your legs, and by the canal the sun is up.

Best chance to push

The last seven kilometres down Jumeirah Street: straight and flat, cheer zones close together, a cooling station near 18 km and the Burj Al Arab growing ahead of you all the way to Sunset Beach.

Three things to know

  1. Entries are first come, first served in price windows, and the last edition sold out; the final window closes on 3 January or sooner at capacity. Official transfers run through the entry platform's dashboard until 3 January. No refunds; deferrals on medical grounds only.
  2. Collect your bib at the expo at Coca-Cola Arena in the three days before; nothing is handed out on race morning. There is no bag drop at the start, so wear a layer you can leave in the charity bins. Take a taxi: the Metro normally opens at eight on Sundays.
  3. Starts go in batches by expected finish time, from sub-1:30 (proof of a time needed) to 3:00, with pacers in every batch. Water every 2.5 km from 5 km, two cooling stations and about 14 cheer zones; the organiser lists no gels. The cut-off is three hours.

Race-day weather

A dark, mild start in the mid-teens, humid at dawn; the sun rises around seven and the coast is warming towards 20°C by the finish. Rain is rare, about one day in eleven in January.

What runners wear

Singlet and shorts, a throwaway layer for the pen, sunglasses for the coast and racing flats: smooth tarmac all the way.

Typical finish times

Course records 59:26 by Joshua Cheptegei in 2026, with the top three men inside four seconds, and 1:06:57 by Irine Cheptai the same morning. Behind them the batches run from sub-1:30 to three hours; 39 per cent of the last field were women.

Worth the trip?

Yes, if you want Dubai's skyline in one run: the Museum of the Future, the Burj Khalifa and the Burj Al Arab inside 21 kilometres, at sunrise, on roads closed for you. The Dubai Marathon is a week earlier. It is a show more than a quiet PB course: two bridges and the turns through Downtown.
