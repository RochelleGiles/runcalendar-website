New York City Marathon

New York · United States

Five boroughs, five bridges, two million people — the runner's guide to the biggest day in marathoning.

Quick verdict

Not a PB course — a life-list course. New York is hillier, harder and louder than any other major, and nobody comes home talking about their splits. You run it for the five boroughs, the bridges, and a Central Park finish you will never forget.

Course at a glance

246mClimbing
5Bridges
5Boroughs

Point-to-point through all five boroughs: over the Verrazzano from Staten Island, the long Brooklyn miles, Queens, the Queensboro Bridge into Manhattan's First Avenue wall of sound, a touch of the Bronx, then Fifth Avenue and the rolling finish in Central Park. About 246m of climbing, nearly all of it bridges.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Queensboro Bridge, miles 15–16

An 800-metre climb in total silence — no spectators allowed — after the noise of Brooklyn. It is the most feared stretch in world marathoning, and the roar when you come off it onto First Avenue is the reward.

Best chance to push

Off the Queensboro onto First Avenue — controlled — and then whatever is left belongs to Central Park's final miles.

Three things to know

  1. Expect to run 5–10 minutes slower than your Berlin/Chicago time — pace by effort on the bridges, not by your watch.
  2. First Avenue is a trap: three-plus miles of wall-of-sound crowds right when you feel heroic. Spend it carefully — Fifth Avenue's long grind is still ahead.
  3. Entry is the hardest of all the majors — the drawing has accepted about 1 in 100 applicants in recent years, with charity, tour and time-qualifier routes beside it. Apply every year, and expect to try more than once.

Race-day weather

Early November, typically 5–14°C — crisp, ideal for the effort, but the bridges are exposed and windy. The start village on Staten Island involves hours of waiting: dress for it.

What runners wear

Throwaway everything for the start village — old jacket, old blanket. Race kit tested in cool weather underneath, and anti-chafe everywhere; the bridges make their own wind.

Typical finish times

The winners run around 2:05–2:10 for the men and 2:20–2:25 for the women on this course. The great pack finishes 4:00–5:00, and the park cheers every single one of them home in the dusk.

Worth the trip?

Unreservedly. If you fly across the world for one marathon in your life, this is the strongest case in the sport — the biggest field, the biggest crowds, the biggest feeling at the finish.
