Queenstown Marathon

Quick verdict

New Zealand's biggest running weekend: 13,000 runners, nearly forty per cent from overseas, on hard-packed trail through gold-rush country to a lakefront finish. The marathon rolls more than the postcard suggests; the half is flatter, faster — and sells out every single year.

Course at a glance

7¾ hrsCutoff
13,000Field

The marathon leaves Millbrook at 8:45, drops through Arrowtown and the Arrow River trail, rounds Lake Hayes, then crosses the Old Shotover Bridge to the Twin Rivers trail, Frankton and the Wakatipu shoreline into Queenstown Gardens. The half starts at Coronet Fields on Malaghans Road and joins the same run home — rivers, lakeshore, Gardens — skipping Arrowtown and Lake Hayes.

Where you'll feel it

📍The rollers around Lake Hayes, mid-marathon.

The marathon's climbing is little and often — about 340 m of short, punchy rises, thickest around Lake Hayes and the Shotover crossings, on legs lulled by the downhill opening from Millbrook. The half's one real rise is the pull up to Old School Road; the rest is honest running.

Best chance to push

Kilometres 25 to 35, the river trails beside the Kawarau: flat, smooth and quiet — the place to lock onto goal pace before the lakefront brings you home. On the half, that same ground is most of the race.

Three things to know

  1. The half has sold out every year since the race began in 2014 — in 2022 it was gone three months out. The marathon holds places longer, and online entry stays open until 5 p.m. the day before if capacity remains.
  2. Both starts are out of town — the marathon at Millbrook, the half at Coronet Fields on Malaghans Road. Shuttles run from central Queenstown from 5:45 a.m. and parking at the starts is minimal: book the bus when you enter, and use the official gear bags for the finish-line drop.
  3. The course closes at 4:30 p.m. — 7¾ hours for the marathon with checkpoint sweeps along the way, about 8½ for the half. At the front it's quick: Jack Moody's 2:25 and Bara Styblova's 2:48 both fell in 2025.

November in Queenstown is spring with opinions: frost-prone 4°C mornings warming toward 18°C, and wind that can swing the day either way. The starts sit inland where the cold pools — the lakefront finish is usually the warmest place on the course.

What runners wear

Throwaway warmth for the start paddocks, racing kit underneath — singlet weather by Frankton on a kind year. Road shoes are right for both races: the trail is hard-packed and smooth, and the road share is ordinary tarmac.

Typical finish times

Records fell in 2025 — 2:25:02 and 2:48:31, with the half's front at 1:10. The 7¾-hour marathon limit is generous and the half's 8½ more so, which is a large part of why 13,000 people keep coming.

Worth the trip?

Yes — this is the occasion New Zealand running builds its spring around, and the scenery advertising is not a lie. Treat entry as a race of its own: the half goes first, flights and beds go next. If you want the full postcard — Arrowtown, Lake Hayes, the rivers — run the marathon.
