Comrades Marathon

Durban, KwaZulu-Natal · South Africa
UltraCountrysideComrades Marathon

The world's oldest ultramarathon — a down-run to the sea through the Valley of a Thousand Hills, with a medal for every kind of brave.

Quick verdict

The oldest and biggest ultra on earth, and the one with a soul. The down run gives back 600 metres but charges for every one of them through the Valley of a Thousand Hills — qualify with a sub-5 marathon, win the ballot, and go.

Course at a glance

↓600mNet drop
44.3kHalfway · Drummond
12 hrsCutoff

Out of Pietermaritzburg in the winter dark, down Polly Shortts inside 8k, then climbing almost unbroken to Umlaas Road at 24k — the course's highest point. Inchanga bites just before 40k, Drummond marks halfway at 44.3k, and then the long fall: Botha's Hill, Field's Hill's three savage kilometres at 64k, Cowies Hill, and the flat last six into Kings Park, Durban.

Where you'll feel it

📍Field's Hill, 64k — a descent, not a climb.

Three kilometres of steep motorway camber your quads have been dreading since dawn. By 64k the down run has already taken most of its 600 metres out of your legs; brake down Field's Hill and you'll walk Pinetown's flat kilometres straight after it.

Best chance to push

From 45th Cutting it's six kilometres of gentle downhill and dead-flat run-in, crowds solid the whole way. Quads intact, you can empty everything into Kings Park.

Three things to know

  1. General entry is a ballot for the first time — 9 to 22 September, drawn on the 23rd, R4,500 international. Enter before you've qualified if you need to: any marathon under 5:00 by 3 May 2027 confirms the place.
  2. Pick your medal before your pace: sub-9 earns a Bill Rowan, sub-11 a bronze, sub-12 a Vic Clapham. Walk the early climbs on purpose — Umlaas Road at 24k is higher than anything that follows, and banked minutes vanish on Field's Hill.
  3. Base yourself in Durban and you run home: buses take you to the Pietermaritzburg start in the small hours. Ten finishes earn a permanent green number — the culture built around that is half of what you're entering.

Race-day weather

Mid-June is KwaZulu-Natal winter: dry and clear, single digits in the Pietermaritzburg dark, low twenties at the Durban finish. The cold start flatters everyone — it's the coastal warmth after 70k that exposes a hard first half.

What runners wear

Throwaway layers for the City Hall wait, then less than the cold suggests — it is warm by mid-morning. Cushioned shoes and downhill-trained quads are the real kit: this race is won by whoever brakes least.

Typical finish times

Winners run about five and a quarter hours. The great mass of the field arrives in the last two medal windows — bronze before 11, Vic Clapham before the gun — so plan for the 12-hour day, not the highlight reel.

Worth the trip?

For anyone drawn to ultras this is the pilgrimage — the oldest, biggest, loudest one on earth, and the down run is the kinder introduction. One caveat: entry is now a ballot, so book flights after the draw on 23 September.
