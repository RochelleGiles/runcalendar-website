Hamburg Marathon

Hamburg · Germany
RoadUrbanMarathon · Half MarathonSold out

Germany's spring marathon: the Reeperbahn in the first two kilometres, the Elbe and the harbour before ten, then the Alster from both banks on the second-fastest course in the country.

Quick verdict

Germany's big spring marathon and its second-fastest course after Berlin: 20,000 marathon places, a Gold Label field that ran 2:03:46 here, and a flat city loop that goes past the Reeperbahn, along the Elbe and round the Außenalster on the way to a red-carpet finish. Most of the field runs the marathon itself; the half starts at two in the afternoon and runs the same final stretch home, and a four-runner relay shares the course. It sells out early, every distance, so the race is entered the spring before. Pick it for a fast time with a city that turns out in numbers.

Course at a glance

~85 mClimbing
8:30Start
6 hCut-off

Two starts, one course. Block A to L leave Karolinenstraße at 8:30, M to Q from Bei den Kirchhöfen half an hour later, and both drop down Glacischaussee to the Reeperbahn by 2 km, through Altona on Königstraße and out along Bernadottestraße to the turn at Othmarschen just past 6 km. The Elbchaussee brings you back east above the river to the Fischmarkt, Landungsbrücken at 10 to 11 km, Baumwall and HafenCity at 13, then the Wallringtunnel into the city: Steinstraße, Ballindamm and the Jungfernstieg around 15 to 16 km. Over the Kennedybrücke, where the half starts, and up the east bank of the Außenalster from 17 to 21 km, past the Stadtpark to Barmbek at 25, City Nord at 26 and 27, Alsterdorf and the long northern loop through Ohlsdorf to the far point at 31 km. Maienweg at 32 turns you for home: Alsterkrugchaussee, Eppendorf at 35 to 37, Klosterstern, then the Harvestehuder Weg down the west bank of the Alster from 38 to 40, Dammtor, Gorch-Fock-Wall and the finish straight on Karolinenstraße. The half runs the east bank with the marathon, cuts north past the Stadtpark instead of looping it, and rejoins at Alsterdorf for Ohlsdorf and the whole run home.

Where you'll feel it

📍City Nord to Ohlsdorf, the quiet loop, kilometres 26–34

Not on a hill: the organiser calls the course almost table-flat with small ramps, and that is true. You feel it between 26 and 34 km, where the route leaves the lake for City Nord and the office streets of Alsterdorf and Ohlsdorf, the crowds thin to a scattering of bands, and the course keeps heading north when every part of you knows the finish is south. The turn at 31 is the real halfway for most people's legs. Then the Alsterkrugchaussee at 33 to 34 is long, straight and open, and if there is any wind on the day this is where you meet it. Hold something back through the Stadtpark and these eight kilometres become a steady grind rather than the place the race got away.

Best chance to push

The Harvestehuder Weg, 38 to 40 km: the lake on your left, the biggest crowds of the day on your right, dead flat and slightly downhill towards Dammtor. The organiser's own course notes say this is where the last reserves get mobilised, and they are right. If Eppendorf at 35 to 37 is the question, this is the answer: let the noise take you to the Gorch-Fock-Wall and the red carpet does the last 400 metres for you.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is first-come and it sells out: all 20,000 marathon places went for this edition, and the half and the relay closed too. Places open the spring before, so enter when they do and train towards it. Your start block comes from the finish time you give at entry, changeable only until the late-March deadline, and the bib arrives by email in mid April.
  2. Race morning runs from the Messehallen. Bibs come from the Expo in Hall A3 on the Friday afternoon and all day Saturday, with race-day collection only through a pre-booked paid slot. Bag drop opens at 7:00 in Hall B6 and returns until 16:00. Pacers run every quarter hour from 3:00 to 5:00, open-ear headphones are the only ones allowed, and water comes every five kilometres from 7.5 km, with bananas from 10, gels from 20 and cola from 25.
  3. The half is a race in its own right: 14:00 from the Alsterglacis by the Kennedybrücke, age 16 and over, pacers from 1:30 to 2:15, a cut-off of 2:45 behind the last starter, and in 2026 more women finished it than men. The relay is four runners on four unequal legs, changing at the Jungfernstieg, City Nord and Maienweg, and the first three can rejoin at Sievekingsplatz to cross the line as a team.

Race-day weather

20,000Field

Late April in Hamburg is cool and changeable: around 7 to 9°C at the 8:30 start, the mid-teens by the time the back of the marathon reaches the Alster, and a different afternoon again for the half at two. April brings measurable rain on roughly one day in three, usually showers between bright spells rather than a wet morning. The city is flat and close to two rivers, so wind counts for more than temperature, and the open northern loop and the Alsterkrugchaussee are where you notice it.

What runners wear

Short sleeves or a singlet with arm warmers you can push down after the Reeperbahn, and a throwaway layer for the corrals on Karolinenstraße, which fill from 7:30 in the shade of the Messehallen. Gloves are a reasonable call at 8°C; a light cap if the forecast is showers. Racing flats: there is nothing on the course to argue with them, and the finish straight is a carpet.

Typical finish times

The course records are 2:03:46 and 2:17:05, the women's set in 2026, and the winners usually come home around 2:04 and 2:18. Behind them the marathon had about 15,500 finishers in 2026 with a six-hour limit that closes the course behind the last starter rather than the gun. The half brought home about 6,800 inside its 2:45 cut-off, and around 1,450 relay teams finished.

Worth the trip?

Yes, for a spring PB on a course that is flat without being dull: a city centre, a harbour, two riverbanks and a lake in one loop, with the crowds of a town that treats the day as a festival. It is not Berlin for scale or history, and the northern loop is plain, but the organisation is German and the finish is one of the best in the country. Enter the day entries open and make a weekend of the Expo, the kids' race and the half on the Sunday afternoon.
