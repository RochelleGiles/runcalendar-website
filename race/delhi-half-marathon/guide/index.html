Delhi Half Marathon

New Delhi, Delhi · India
RoadUrbanHalf Marathon · 10K

Asia's fastest half and Delhi's biggest morning: 40,000 across the day on the flattest, widest roads in the country, past India Gate and up Kartavya Path before the sun is properly up.

Quick verdict

A World Athletics Gold Label half marathon that is also the capital's biggest morning out: around 40,000 people across the day, 14,000 of them in the half and nearly 10,000 in the 10K. The course is as flat as a road race gets — 19 metres of climbing, two gentle flyovers — and it runs the best stretch of tarmac in India, Kartavya Path towards Rashtrapati Bhavan. Entries closed in September; if you hold one, the race is won or lost on how you handle the 5 am humidity and the second flyover at 19 km.

Course at a glance

5:00Start
3:30Cut-off

Out of Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium onto Lodhi Road in the dark — flat, wide and lined with the loudest crowds of the morning. A U-turn near 2.6 km brings you back past Safdarjung's Tomb and Lodhi Garden, then the Mathura Road flyover around 6 km, a gentle rise down towards India Gate. From 8 to 11 km you are on Kartavya Path with Rashtrapati Bhavan filling the horizon, the widest, smoothest road in the country. A turn at Sansad Marg, where the 10K joins, then Janpath, a sharper U-turn near 13.5 km, a loop of India Gate, and the flyover again just short of 19 km. The last two kilometres are Lodhi Road once more, flat and loud, with the final 100 metres inside the stadium. The 10K starts on Sansad Marg at 7:30 and runs the half's last ten kilometres.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Mathura Road flyover, second pass, just before 19 km

The Mathura Road flyover the second time, just before 19 km. It is only a few metres of climb, but it comes after an hour and a half of heat and humidity, on a narrow approach where the field bunches, and runners who push the rise pay for it on Lodhi Road. Earlier, the merge with the 10K around 12 km can put a wall of people in front of you — hold your line and your rhythm. Nothing else on the course goes up.

Best chance to push

Kartavya Path, 8 to 11 km: the widest road you will ever race on, dead flat, with Rashtrapati Bhavan ahead of you and the sun just coming up behind. Settle into goal pace here and let it carry you through India Gate. Then, if the flyover left anything in the legs, the last two kilometres of Lodhi Road are flat and lined the whole way — that is where the fast finishes happen.

Three things to know

  1. It is warm and humid even at 5 am — around 23°C with humidity near 80% — and anyone out past two and a half hours is running into real heat. Set a goal for a warm day, not a cool one.
  2. Entry is by timing certificate and your certificate sets your wave, A to F; the half starts at 5:00 with corrals strictly segregated, so be at the stadium by 4 am. The Metro runs free from 3:15 am to JLN Stadium and Jor Bagh.
  3. Delhi's October air is the race's known problem. The organiser moved the date out of Diwali week and the start to before dawn, and sprays and mist zones line the course — but check the air quality index that morning and run to effort, not to your watch.

Mid-October in Delhi: around 23°C at the 5:00 start and 26°C when the 10K goes off at 7:30, humidity near 80% before sunrise at about 6:20, and no rain worth planning for. The air quality is the thing to watch — October is when Delhi's pollution climbs, and the pre-dawn start exists partly for that reason.

What runners wear

The lightest sweat-wicking singlet and shorts you own, never cotton; a cap for the sun once you are on Kartavya Path and India Gate, and sunscreen before the start. Racing flats — the roads are wide, smooth and flat, with one short cobbled stretch near 1 km on Lodhi Road.

Typical finish times

The course records are 58:53 and 1:04:46, and the winners usually come home under the hour. Behind them the amateur half's waves say the rest: A is under 1:50, C runs 2:10 to 2:30, and the biggest waves, D to F, finish between 2:30 and the 3:30 cut-off. The 10K cut-off is 1:30 from its start. India's own best runners go under 1:05 here.

Worth the trip?

For the running, yes — it is the fastest half in Asia, a Gold Label field and a course past the most famous landmarks in India. For the air, be honest with yourself: mid-October Delhi is the wrong week for a personal best if you are sensitive to it. Overseas entries cost about US$60 and need a timing certificate; the window opens in July and the half fills by early September.
