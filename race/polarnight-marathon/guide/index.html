Midnight Sun Marathon

Tromsø, Troms · Norway
RoadCoastalMarathon · Half Marathon · 10K · 5KEntries open

A Saturday-night marathon 350 km north of the Arctic Circle: twice over the Tromsø Bridge, along the fjord, and across the line with the sun still up.

Quick verdict

The world's northernmost AIMS-certified marathon, and the only one you finish in daylight after midnight. Saturday 19 June 2027 in Tromsø, five distances from a kids' dash to the full 42.195 km, close to 9,000 runners from 97 nations in 2026, and a course that never rises above 43 m except for the bridge. Entries are open now. It is a race for the experience more than the time — though the sea-level air and the cool night have produced a 2:20 — and the whole town stays up to see you home.

Course at a glance

Start and finish on the waterfront in the city centre. The marathon crosses the Tromsø Bridge onto the mainland, past the Arctic Cathedral, and comes back over it — two crossings of the span are the course's only climbs, from 6 m by the harbour to 43 m at the crown — with the rest on the island's shoreline roads under the mountains across the water. Kilometre markers count down to the finish; aid stations with water, sports drink and bananas come roughly every 5 km, with Red Cross posts beside them.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Tromsø Bridge: a long, gentle ramp to 43 m above the water, taken in each direction. That is the whole climb.

On the bridge ramps, twice, and in your head: it is light the whole way, the sun sits low and gold over the fjord rather than setting, and your body has no idea what time it is. Runners who nap in the afternoon and eat a proper early dinner run the second half far better than those who treat it as a normal evening.

Best chance to push

The shoreline kilometres on the island after the second bridge crossing: flat, sheltered from the worst of the wind, and lit like early evening. A negative split is realistic here if you paced the bridges sensibly.

Three things to know

  1. Entries for 19 June 2027 are open at msm.no. The limits are generous — 5 h 30 for the marathon, 3 h 30 for the half — but the finish line closes at 02:00, so the marathon is an evening start whatever your pace.
  2. The organiser found that the 2026 courses were 143.6 m short, so 2026 times stand for placings only; the courses are AIMS control-measured and the records date from 2022 and 2025.
  3. Bib collection and the expo run Friday and Saturday in town. The half, the 10 km and the 4.2 km mini marathon share the night, so families and non-running partners have their own start line.

June in Tromsø is cool rather than cold: typically 8–14°C through the evening, sometimes down to 5°C with a wind off the fjord after midnight, and rain is always possible. The sun is up all night but low, so the light is golden and the warmth is not; the organiser's own line is that they cannot guarantee sunshine.

What runners wear

Shorts and a long-sleeve or a light tee with arm sleeves for most; a gilet or light jacket for the second half is common, and sunglasses and a cap are genuinely useful at midnight. Dress for a cool autumn evening at home, not for summer.

Typical finish times

The course records are 2:20:03 (Ebrahim Abdulaziz, 2022) and 2:36:11 (Marte Mæhlum Johansen, 2025). Behind them it is a tourist field: 1,537 marathon finishers in 2025 across a wide spread, with the 5 h 30 limit bringing the back home around 02:00.

Worth the trip?

Yes, and plan more than the race: Tromsø in June is a place to walk at 1 a.m., take the cable car up Fjellheisen for the midnight view, and sail the fjords. A weekend is enough for the race; a week is enough for the Arctic summer. Pair it with the Polar Night Marathon in January if you want the other half of the year.
