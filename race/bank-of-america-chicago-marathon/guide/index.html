Chicago Marathon

Chicago, Illinois · United States
RoadUrbanMarathon

Flat, loud and electric — a runner's guide to the people's world-record course.

Quick verdict

The friendliest fast course in the world. A pancake-flat lap of 29 neighbourhoods, 1.7 million people cheering, and the fastest marathon ever run. Chicago gives you a big-city major with none of the hills and all of the noise.

Course at a glance

<10mClimbing
FlatProfile
29Neighbourhoods

Start and finish in Grant Park on the lakefront, looping north, west and south through the city grid — Lincoln Park, the Loop, Little Italy, Pilsen, Chinatown — before the run home up Michigan Avenue. Under 10 metres of meaningful climbing; seven world records, including the fastest marathon ever recorded.

Where you'll feel it

📍Miles 1–8, the North Side

Not because they're hard, but because they're too easy. The crowds are enormous, the adrenaline is free, and every Chicago mistake is made in the first hour. The course only shows its teeth in the last 8k, when the early enthusiasm sends its invoice.

Best chance to push

From 35k, up Michigan Avenue — flat, straight, and lined with noise. If your first hour was patient, this is payday.

Three things to know

  1. The danger here is the start, not the finish — the crowd energy in the early miles seduces almost everyone into banking time. Don't.
  2. October in Chicago is 7–15°C and famously breezy — ideal for racing, but plan a throwaway layer and check the wind the night before.
  3. Entry is by lottery, with charity and tour-operator routes as backup. Chinatown around the 30k mark is the roar to look forward to.

Race-day weather

Typically 7–15°C with a lake breeze — cool, ideal racing weather. The wind is the only variable worth watching.

What runners wear

Vest and shorts from 12°C up; gloves and arm warmers below 10°C. Throwaway layer for Grant Park at dawn.

Typical finish times

Winners run low-2:00s — the world's fastest time was set here. The heart of the field finishes 3:45–4:45, and the party in Grant Park runs long past the last finisher.

Worth the trip?

Yes — if Berlin is the purist's fast major, Chicago is the joyful one. Same PB potential, twice the noise, and an American big-city weekend around it. A perfect first major.
