Mallorca by UTMB

Sóller, Balearic Islands · Spain
UltraMountain138 km · 104 km · 56 km · 26 km

Four races across the UNESCO Serra de Tramuntana — a night start by the sea, dry-stone pilgrim paths, and every one of them finishing at Port de Sóller.

Quick verdict

The Tramuntana is the sharpest scenery in the Mediterranean and this is the hardest way to see it: technical limestone, cobbled pilgrim staircases and 1,200-metre cols, with every race ending at the same small port. Third edition, already a World Series fixture. Young race, serious mountains.

Course at a glance

5,350mClimbing
30½ hrsCutoff
2,300Field

Every race runs point-to-point through the Serra de Tramuntana to Port de Sóller. The 138 km flagship leaves Cala d'Estellencs at 22:00 on Friday and climbs through the night — Esporles, Valldemossa, Orient, Alaró — before the high ground at Tossals Verds and Cúber, Lluc's sanctuary, the flanks of 1,436 m Puig Major, and the long cobbled drop through Fornalutx to the sea.

Where you'll feel it

📍The Barranc de Biniaraix, in the closing kilometres.

A cobbled pilgrim staircase dropping stone step after stone step off the ridge, protected as a cultural monument since 1994 and no kinder for it. It arrives when your quads are already spent, on every course that crosses the high ground. Nothing about it lets you stride.

Best chance to push

The finish valley. Off the mountain at Fornalutx the course finally gives you runnable ground, dropping through the valley to the harbour front at Port de Sóller — the one stretch where the island stops fighting back.

Three things to know

  1. Three of the four races need a valid UTMB Index at the distance — only the 26 km is open to anyone. Entry is first-come rather than a lottery, and finishing banks Running Stones for the Mont-Blanc draw: four on the 138 km down to one on the 26.
  2. The 138 km starts at 22:00 on Friday — a full night on technical limestone before first light. The kit list is full UTMB spec: 10,000 mm waterproof, thermals, two torches, survival bag — and proof of mountain-rescue insurance is checked at bib collection.
  3. No race starts where it finishes: free organiser buses connect Port de Sóller to the three start villages. Parts of the course cross private estates opened only on race day, so the full route cannot be recced beforehand.

October is Mallorca's wettest month — around 70 mm falling in short violent bursts — while the coast still sits at 21–23°C. On the 1,200 m cols, night cold and channelled wind are a different climate; the kit list exists because both can happen in one race.

What runners wear

Start dressed for a warm Mediterranean evening and carry the mountain kit anyway — the waterproof, thermals and gloves are mandatory because storms build fast over the ridge. Grip matters more than cushion on wet limestone and cobbles.

Typical finish times

Hannes Namberger won the 2025 138 km in 13:37; the last finishers used nearly all of the 30½ hours, arriving in Sunday's small hours after two nights out. On the 56 km the winner ran 5:04 and the middle of the field around 8:30–9:00 against a 12-hour limit.

Worth the trip?

Yes — this is the rare island race with real mountains in it. A UNESCO cultural landscape end to end, village squares and a sanctuary for checkpoints, and an October date that stretches the season after the Alps have closed. The Index rule shuts most doors; the 26 km leaves one open for everyone.
