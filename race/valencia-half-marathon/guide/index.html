Valencia Half Marathon

València, Valencia · Spain
RoadUrbanHalf MarathonEntries open

The fastest half marathon on earth — both world records were run here — on a flat loop from the City of Arts and Sciences through the old town and back.

Quick verdict

The half marathon where the world records live: 57:30 and 1:02:52 were both run on these streets. Flat, at sea level, 25,000 runners, a late-October morning that starts cool and warms, and a finish beside the City of Arts and Sciences. This year's race is full; entries for 2027 open within days of race day and the 2026 edition sold out within a month. Save it as Interested and we'll tell you the morning it opens.

Course at a glance

~0Climbing
2:45Cutoff
25,000Field

Starts and finishes on Carrer d'Antonio Ferrandis, a kilometre from the City of Arts and Sciences, and crosses that landmark in the opening kilometres. Wide avenues through the Ensanche and out to the Maritime Quarter, then the long run along the Turia gardens — the old riverbed that is now the city's green spine — north-west into the old town, Ciutat Vella, and back down the same corridor to the finish. Aid at 5, 10, 15 and 19K.

Where you'll feel it

📍There isn't one — the course is flat end to end.

Nowhere, and that is the trap. With no hill to set a rhythm against, the only thing that slows you is the pace you chose in the first 5K on a course that makes every pace feel easy. The old town's streets narrow slightly around 15K, and if you went out on record pace that is where you find out.

Best chance to push

The last 6K along the Turia gardens back toward the City of Arts and Sciences: straight, wide, downhill by a whisker and lined all the way. If you have paced the first 15K, this is where a Valencia PB is made.

Three things to know

  1. Entries for 2027 open in the days after this year's race and go first-come: the 2026 edition opened on 30 October and closed on 30 November, the first 10,000 bibs at €40 and the rest at €80. Save the race as Interested and we'll tell you the morning it opens.
  2. Seven waves from 8:25 to 9:27 by predicted time, with a 2:45 limit from your own start — generous, but the day warms quickly, so an early wave is worth an honest prediction.
  3. It is one of the SuperHalfs with Lisbon, Prague, Copenhagen and Cardiff, and a Gold Label race — if you are collecting the series, this is the one people plan a personal best around.

Late October in Valencia: 14–16°C at the 8:25 start, often 20°C or more by the finish, dry far more often than not, with a sea breeze on the Maritime Quarter stretch. Warm years are the ones that catch the back of the field.

What runners wear

Singlet and shorts, no question — even the elites race light here. A cap or sunglasses for the second half, and nothing for the start line: the corrals are warm by the time you cross.

Typical finish times

World records are 57:30 (Yomif Kejelcha, 2024) and 1:02:52 (Letesenbet Gidey, 2021), both run here. 25,000 places, and a field that runs faster than any other half of its size — sub-1:30 is ordinary in the front third.

Worth the trip?

Yes. If a half-marathon time matters to you, this is the course to run it on, in a city built for a long weekend: beach, old town and the City of Arts and Sciences within a few kilometres of the start. It is the SuperHalfs' fast one, and it knows it.
