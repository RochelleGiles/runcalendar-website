Lisbon Half Marathon

Lisboa, Lisbon · Portugal
RoadHistoric-streetsHalf Marathon · 10KSold out

The fastest half marathon course in the world: down off the 25 de Abril Bridge, out along the Tagus and home to Jerónimos, with four men's world records set on it.

Quick verdict

The fastest half marathon course there is, and one of the great starts in road running: 20,000 people on the upper deck of the 25 de Abril Bridge, the Tagus seventy metres below, and the whole race downhill or flat from there. Four men's world records have been set on it, most recently 57:20 in 2026. Behind the elites it is a PB course with one honest catch: two long out-and-backs on the river road, where the wind decides your second half. The 10K starts on the same bridge an hour later and shares the finish at Jerónimos. Places go by ballot the spring before and the race fills.

You start at the toll plaza on the south bank at Almada, the highest point of the morning, and the first two kilometres are the bridge itself: a gentle rise to mid-span, then the long fall towards Lisbon with the city and the river opening up on both sides. The ramps bring you down into Alcântara by about 4 km and the course turns east along the waterfront towards Cais do Sodré, the first turn of the day, then back the way you came. From Alcântara it is the river road west: the docks, the Padrão dos Descobrimentos and the Torre de Belém, then on through Algés and Dafundo to the far turn at Cruz Quebrada, and the same road home to the finish in front of the Jerónimos Monastery. The 10K crosses the bridge behind you at 10:15 and runs the river road straight to the same finish.

Where you'll feel it

📍The river road out to Cruz Quebrada, into the wind, kilometres 13–18

There is no climb to speak of, so the course finds you another way. The bridge gives you the fastest first four kilometres you will ever run, and a 9:20 start with 20,000 on a two-lane river road means the pace is set by the crowd around you, not by you. Then the turns: Cais do Sodré and Cruz Quebrada are both out-and-backs along the open Tagus, and whichever way the wind is blowing you will meet it head-on for one of the long legs, usually the stretch out to Cruz Quebrada past 13 km, when the downhill is long gone and the sun is up. The last three kilometres back into Belém are flat and lined, but by then the day decides how they feel.

Best chance to push

Off the bridge. The descent from mid-span into Alcântara is nearly four kilometres of free speed, and the runners who finish well are the ones who let it set a rhythm rather than a split. Then the run home from Cruz Quebrada: if the westerly was in your face on the way out it is on your back now, the Torre de Belém marks three to go, and the finish straight in front of the monastery is wide, flat and loud.

Three things to know

  1. Entry is a ballot, drawn the spring before: applications for this edition ran from late April to mid May the previous year, free to enter, up to three runners on one application, 18 or over on race day. The drawn pay within three days or lose the place, and when the 20,000 are gone the organiser opens a waiting list. SuperHalfs passport holders skip the ballot; charity and official tour-operator places are the other door.
  2. The start is reached one way only: the Fertagus train to Pragal, free with your bib, and spectators are not allowed on the bridge. Your start box is printed on the bib. There is no bag drop of any kind, for either race, so you wear what you race in plus something to throw away, and carry nothing you want back. A proven time under 1:10 for men or 1:25 for women gets you a front-box bib from the Solutions desk at the Expo.
  3. Kit comes only from the Sport Expo at the Centro de Congressos de Lisboa on the Thursday, Friday and Saturday, 10:00 to 20:00; nothing is handed out on race day or posted. Seven drinks stations, four music stages and two medical posts line the course, and your bib is a free ticket on the Metro, Carris buses and CP trains until 16:00, which is how you get back from Belém.

Early March in Lisbon is cool at the start and mild by the finish: around 11 to 13°C on the bridge at 9:20, and the high teens by the time the back of the field reaches Belém. March averages about eight wet days, so roughly one race morning in four sees rain, usually a passing shower rather than a soaking. The variable that matters is the wind off the Tagus: the river road is open for its whole length, and a westerly turns the Cruz Quebrada leg into work.

What runners wear

A singlet or short sleeves and shorts for the race, plus a layer you are happy to lose at the start: there is no bag drop, and the toll plaza at Almada is high, exposed and often windy for the hour before the gun. Arm warmers earn their place on the river road; a cap for the sun once it is over Belém. Racing flats or your fastest road shoes: the surface is tarmac throughout, with nothing technical anywhere.

Typical finish times

The course records are 57:20 and 1:04:21, both set in the last two years, and the men's time was a world record on the day; the winners usually come home under the hour and around 1:05. Behind them the race is run for a PB: the cap is 20,000 and the three-hour limit catches almost nobody. The 10K has 9,500 places and a limit of 2:20 from its 10:15 start.

Worth the trip?

Yes, if you want a fast half with one unforgettable moment rather than a city tour: the course is a bridge and a river road, not Lisbon's hills and trams, and the second half is an out-and-back. But the start is like nothing else in the sport, the finish is a UNESCO monastery, it carries a SuperHalfs stamp, and the weekend falls when the city is quiet and cheap to reach. Enter the ballot the spring before, or book the week off and come back for the 10K.
